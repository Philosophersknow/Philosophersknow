## Three Things Worth Knowing

### 1. The DRMC: A New Framework for Local Democracy

The Dynamic Relational Model of Consciousness (DRMC) is a governance and intelligence framework developed over ten years of research, professional practice, and direct engagement with local government in Australia.

Its premise is deceptively simple: **consciousness is not sealed inside individual minds**. It is shaped through bodies, places, histories, institutions, language, technology, economics, ecology, culture and power.

What does that mean for local government?

It means that when a council makes a decision about a road, it is never only making a decision about a road. It may also be making a decision about disability access, economic participation, climate responsibility, public trust, community identity, and the memory of past decisions. A development approval is never only about built form. It may also be about local character, ecological anxiety, housing pressure, inherited distrust, and the feeling that a place is being rewritten without consent.

**The DRMC asks councils — and the communities they serve — to face those questions directly.**

The DRMC Council Benchmarking Project applies this framework to local government performance. It goes beyond what councils report to examine what communities actually experience. It recognises that community experience is evidence — not a substitute for formal data, but an equal partner to it.

The full framework is set out in *DRMC Council Benchmarking: Local Government Is Where Democracy Becomes Visible* — available as a complete course and published text.

**The DRMC 26-Domain Matrix covers:**
- Financial performance and fiscal ethics
- Community trust and belonging
- Place identity and ecological stewardship
- Consultation quality and public reasoning
- Democratic participation and voice
- Intergenerational responsibility
- Ethical coherence and institutional integrity
- And nineteen further relational domains

### 2. AI in Further Education: The Global Picture and What It Means for Your Organisation

The UK further education sector — and vocational education systems internationally — are at a pivotal moment with AI.

AI is already changing how students learn, how assessors verify competency, how training organisations manage administrative workload, and what employers expect from graduates. The providers that are managing this well share one characteristic: they have made deliberate, governed decisions about AI. They have not banned it or ignored it. They have understood it.

The briefing *AI in Further Education and Skills: International Comparisons and What UK Providers Need to Do Now* draws on research across twelve jurisdictions — the UK, Ireland, Finland, Canada, New Zealand, Singapore, China, Japan, the EU, Indonesia, Australia and the United States.

**Key findings for UK FE providers:**
- Staff confidence, not technology, is the primary adoption barrier in UK colleges
- The UK has strong AI principles but uneven implementation across the sector
- Ofsted's 2025 early-adopter study confirmed: "the biggest risk is doing nothing"
- A regulatory gap exists between what is expected of universities (via OfS) and what is expected of FE colleges — but this gap is closing
- Skills England's April 2026 AI leadership apprenticeship units signal that qualifications infrastructure is already changing

The full report (27,000 words, twelve country comparisons, practical provider recommendations, regulator recommendations) is available as a paid briefing.
