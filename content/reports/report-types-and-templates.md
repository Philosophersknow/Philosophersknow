# AIIPD Civic Intelligence™ — Report Types and Templates

**Purpose:** Complete taxonomy of AIIPD intelligence products. Provides naming conventions, descriptions, audience guidance, structural templates, and cadence recommendations for every intelligence report type in the platform.

---

## REPORT TYPE 1: COUNCIL INTELLIGENCE PROFILE

### 1. Old Name (Benchmarking Era)
Council Performance Report / Council Scorecard

### 2. New Name (Intelligence Era)
Council Intelligence Profile

### 3. Description
The Council Intelligence Profile is AIIPD's flagship organisational intelligence product — a comprehensive, multi-dimensional intelligence portrait of a local government organisation. It integrates governance, financial, community, infrastructure, environmental, and strategic intelligence into a coherent analytical picture of the organisation's current state, trajectory, and context. The Profile draws on all available evidence dimensions — from official published documents to comparative jurisdiction data — and applies the DRMC framework to produce an analytically grounded, interpretively rich intelligence picture. It is designed to be useful to the full executive leadership team, providing both a shared understanding of the organisation's situation and a foundation for strategic planning, governance improvement, and evidence-based decision-making.

### 4. Primary Audience
Local Government CEO, Executive Leadership Team, Mayor and Elected Members (adapted version), State Government Agency Partners

### 5. Key Intelligence Dimensions Covered
- Governance intelligence (structures, decision patterns, accountability, transparency)
- Financial intelligence (sustainability, trajectory, comparative financial position)
- Community intelligence (demographics, need, satisfaction, social cohesion)
- Infrastructure intelligence (asset condition, investment patterns, gap analysis)
- Workforce intelligence (capacity, capability, culture indicators)
- Environmental intelligence (sustainability performance, climate risk, environmental obligations)
- Strategic intelligence (plan quality, implementation evidence, strategic coherence)

### 6. Suggested Structure and Sections
1. Executive Intelligence Summary (2 pages)
2. Organisational Context and Characteristics
3. Governance Intelligence Analysis
4. Financial Intelligence Analysis
5. Community Intelligence Analysis
6. Infrastructure Intelligence Analysis
7. Workforce Intelligence Overview
8. Environmental Intelligence Overview
9. Strategic Intelligence Assessment
10. Comparative Place Intelligence Context
11. Emerging Signals and Strategic Considerations
12. Intelligence Sources and Methodology

### 7. Recommended Cadence
Annual (aligned with financial year reporting cycle), with a mid-year Intelligence Refresh for strategic planning contexts.

### 8. Visual Treatment Notes
Professional document design with clear section navigation. Cover page: organisation name, LGA image, intelligence date. Section dividers with domain colour coding. Intelligence narrative supported by data visualisations (trend charts, comparative position diagrams, spider charts for multi-dimensional views). No numerical score on cover or summary page. Executive summary as a standalone extract designed for elected member distribution.

## REPORT TYPE 2: PLACE INTELLIGENCE REPORT

### 1. Old Name (Benchmarking Era)
Area Profile / Geographic Performance Summary

### 2. New Name (Intelligence Era)
Place Intelligence Report

### 3. Description
The Place Intelligence Report provides a comprehensive intelligence portrait of a defined geographic area — a local government area, suburb, region, or custom-defined place boundary. Unlike the Council Intelligence Profile, which focuses on the organisation, the Place Intelligence Report focuses on the place itself — its people, conditions, dynamics, and trajectory. It integrates demographic intelligence, economic indicators, social conditions, environmental data, infrastructure landscape, governance context, and comparative place analysis into a structured evidence picture of what is happening in a place and why it matters. The report is designed for anyone who needs to understand a place before making decisions about it — whether those decisions involve investment, service design, planning, policy, or community engagement.

### 4. Primary Audience
Infrastructure and Planning Professionals, Investors and Funding Bodies, State Government Agencies, Policy Makers, Consultants and Researchers, Community Organisations

### 5. Key Intelligence Dimensions Covered
- Demographic intelligence (population, age profile, diversity, growth patterns)
- Economic intelligence (employment, industry, economic activity, development indicators)
- Social intelligence (community wellbeing, social cohesion, vulnerability indices)
- Infrastructure intelligence (existing infrastructure, gaps, planned investment)
- Environmental intelligence (land use, natural assets, hazard exposure)
- Governance context (local government capacity, service delivery, governance quality)
- Comparative place intelligence (similar places, regional context)

### 6. Suggested Structure and Sections
1. Place Intelligence Summary (2 pages)
2. Place Definition and Geographic Context
3. Demographic Intelligence
4. Economic and Employment Intelligence
5. Social and Community Intelligence
6. Infrastructure and Built Environment Intelligence
7. Environmental and Natural Assets Intelligence
8. Governance and Service Context
9. Comparative Place Analysis
10. Emerging Place Signals
11. Strategic Implications for Stakeholders
12. Intelligence Sources

### 7. Recommended Cadence
Produced on demand for specific planning, investment, or policy purposes. Annual update for high-priority or high-investment areas.

### 8. Visual Treatment Notes
Map-led design with place imagery. Cover page with LGA or region map highlighting the defined place. Strong use of spatial visualisations — population density maps, service access maps, infrastructure condition layers. Demographic data presented as population pyramids, trend charts, and comparative tables. Executive summary designed for non-technical stakeholders. Infographic summary page for community-facing contexts.

## REPORT TYPE 3: GOVERNANCE INTELLIGENCE REVIEW

### 1. Old Name (Benchmarking Era)
Governance Compliance Report / Governance Performance Review

### 2. New Name (Intelligence Era)
Governance Intelligence Review

### 3. Description
The Governance Intelligence Review is AIIPD's in-depth analysis of an organisation's governance structures, decision-making patterns, accountability mechanisms, transparency practices, and organisational health indicators. It is designed as a tool for governance improvement — not audit or compliance — providing executive leadership and elected officials with a nuanced, evidence-based understanding of how their organisation governs itself and how that governance shapes organisational and community outcomes. The review draws on meeting minutes, governance documentation, audit records, policy frameworks, decision histories, and comparative governance intelligence from similar organisations to produce an analytically grounded governance picture. It identifies both governance strengths and areas where governance quality could be enhanced, supported by evidence and practical recommendations.

### 4. Primary Audience
Mayor and Elected Members, CEO and Executive Leadership Team, Governance and Legal Advisors, State Government Oversight Bodies

### 5. Key Intelligence Dimensions Covered
- Governance structures and frameworks
- Decision-making patterns and quality
- Transparency and accountability mechanisms
- Elected member-executive relationship intelligence
- Meeting quality and engagement patterns
- Policy coherence and strategic alignment
- Probity and integrity environment
- Community trust and governance legitimacy indicators
- Comparative governance intelligence

### 6. Suggested Structure and Sections
1. Governance Intelligence Summary
2. Governance Framework Overview
3. Decision-Making Pattern Analysis
4. Transparency and Accountability Assessment
5. Elected Member Engagement Intelligence
6. Executive Governance Quality
7. Community Trust and Legitimacy Indicators
8. Comparative Governance Context
9. Governance Improvement Opportunities
10. Intelligence Sources and Methodology

### 7. Recommended Cadence
Annual governance intelligence review. Triggered review following significant governance events (elections, CEO transitions, major policy decisions, public controversies).

### 8. Visual Treatment Notes
Authoritative document style — restrained, professional, legal-adjacent aesthetics. No RAG scoring on governance dimensions. Narrative-led analysis with evidence citations. Governance framework diagrams where they aid comprehension. Executive summary suitable for closed session council briefing.

## REPORT TYPE 4: COMMUNITY INTELLIGENCE PROFILE

### 1. Old Name (Benchmarking Era)
Community Satisfaction Report / Community Survey Results

### 2. New Name (Intelligence Era)
Community Intelligence Profile

### 3. Description
The Community Intelligence Profile is AIIPD's comprehensive intelligence product focused on the people, social conditions, and community dynamics of a local government area. It moves well beyond satisfaction surveys to integrate demographic data, social indicator analysis, consultation synthesis, community needs assessment, and comparative community intelligence into a rich, multi-dimensional understanding of a community. The Profile is designed to support strategic planning, service design, community engagement, and advocacy — providing the evidence base that authentic community understanding requires. It captures not only what is happening in a community now, but the demographic and social trajectories that will shape the community's future and the intelligence implications of those trajectories for governance and service planning.

### 4. Primary Audience
Local Government CEO, Director of Community Services, Strategic Planners, Community Organisations, Mayor and Elected Members, State Government Policy Makers

### 5. Key Intelligence Dimensions Covered
- Demographic profile and trajectory
- Social cohesion and community wellbeing
- Service access and adequacy
- Community needs and vulnerability
- Community consultation synthesis
- Cultural diversity and inclusion intelligence
- Community safety and resilience
- Youth, ageing, and life-stage intelligence
- Comparative community intelligence

### 6. Suggested Structure and Sections
1. Community Intelligence Summary
2. Demographic Profile and Trajectory
3. Social Cohesion and Wellbeing Intelligence
4. Service Access and Adequacy Analysis
5. Community Needs and Vulnerability Intelligence
6. Community Consultation Synthesis
7. Cultural and Diversity Intelligence
8. Youth and Ageing Intelligence
9. Community Safety and Resilience
10. Comparative Community Context
11. Strategic Implications for Planning and Services
12. Intelligence Sources

### 7. Recommended Cadence
Annual update aligned with strategic planning cycle. Significant update every three to five years for community strategic plan development. Population-triggered review (for high-growth or high-change communities).

### 8. Visual Treatment Notes
Warm, accessible document design. Strong use of demographic visualisations — population pyramids, age distribution charts, diversity maps. Community photography (with consent). Infographic summary page for community publication. Plain language executive summary suitable for community engagement contexts.

## REPORT TYPE 5: COMPARATIVE PLACE INTELLIGENCE ASSESSMENT

### 1. Old Name (Benchmarking Era)
Comparative Performance Report / Peer Benchmarking Report

### 2. New Name (Intelligence Era)
Comparative Place Intelligence Assessment

### 3. Description
The Comparative Place Intelligence Assessment provides structured comparative intelligence across a defined group of organisations or places — revealing patterns, variations, shared challenges, and divergent approaches that are only visible when multiple jurisdictions are examined simultaneously. Unlike rankings or scorecards, the Comparative Assessment focuses on understanding variation — what contextual factors explain different outcomes, what approaches are producing strong results in specific settings, and where common challenges suggest the need for shared or coordinated responses. The assessment is designed for both individual organisational use (understanding your own position in context) and for state government, policy, and regional bodies who need intelligence across a group of jurisdictions.

### 4. Primary Audience
Local Government CEO and Executive Leadership, State Government Agencies, Regional Planning Bodies, Policy Makers, Management Consultants

### 5. Key Intelligence Dimensions Covered
- Comparative governance intelligence
- Comparative financial intelligence
- Comparative community intelligence
- Comparative infrastructure intelligence
- Comparative strategic intelligence
- Contextual similarity and difference analysis
- Pattern identification across jurisdictions
- Emerging comparative signals

### 6. Suggested Structure and Sections
1. Comparative Intelligence Summary
2. Comparator Group Definition and Rationale
3. Contextual Similarity Analysis
4. Comparative Governance Intelligence
5. Comparative Financial Intelligence
6. Comparative Community Intelligence
7. Comparative Infrastructure Intelligence
8. Pattern Intelligence: What the Comparison Reveals
9. Divergent Approaches and Outcomes Worth Understanding
10. Shared Challenges and Potential Collaborative Responses
11. Intelligence Implications for [Primary Organisation or Purpose]
12. Intelligence Sources and Methodology

### 7. Recommended Cadence
Annual comparative assessment for strategic planning contexts. Triggered assessments for specific strategic questions or policy evaluations.

### 8. Visual Treatment Notes
Comparative visualisation as the primary design language — side-by-side comparisons, scatter plots with contextual labels, grouped bar charts showing variation. No ranked lists. Organisations identified by name but positioned in a landscape, not a league table. Narrative interpretation prominent alongside visual comparisons.

## REPORT TYPE 6: INFRASTRUCTURE INTELLIGENCE BRIEF

### 1. Old Name (Benchmarking Era)
Infrastructure Performance Report / Capital Works Comparison

### 2. New Name (Intelligence Era)
Infrastructure Intelligence Brief

### 3. Description
The Infrastructure Intelligence Brief provides decision-grade intelligence on an organisation's infrastructure landscape — integrating asset condition analysis, maintenance investment patterns, capital works program assessment, infrastructure gap identification, demand forecasting, and comparative infrastructure intelligence from comparable jurisdictions. The Brief is designed to support high-stakes infrastructure investment decisions, long-term asset management planning, capital works prioritisation, and infrastructure grant applications. It provides executives and technical directors with the evidence they need to justify infrastructure investment decisions to elected members and funding bodies, and to manage the long-term infrastructure obligations that represent one of local government's most significant financial and operational responsibilities.

### 4. Primary Audience
Director of Infrastructure, Engineering, or Works; CFO; CEO; State Government Infrastructure Agencies; Funding Bodies

### 5. Key Intelligence Dimensions Covered
- Asset condition intelligence
- Maintenance investment analysis
- Capital works program assessment
- Infrastructure gap analysis
- Demand and growth forecasting
- Financial sustainability of infrastructure investment
- Comparative infrastructure intelligence
- Emerging infrastructure risk signals

### 6. Suggested Structure and Sections
1. Infrastructure Intelligence Summary
2. Asset Portfolio Overview
3. Asset Condition Intelligence
4. Maintenance Investment Analysis
5. Capital Works Program Assessment
6. Infrastructure Gap Analysis
7. Demand and Growth Intelligence
8. Financial Sustainability Analysis
9. Comparative Infrastructure Intelligence
10. Emerging Infrastructure Risk Signals
11. Strategic Recommendations for Infrastructure Planning
12. Intelligence Sources

### 7. Recommended Cadence
Annual infrastructure intelligence brief. Project-triggered brief for major capital investment decisions. Grant-application brief for infrastructure funding applications.

### 8. Visual Treatment Notes
Technical but accessible design. Asset category icons for navigation. Condition heat maps and investment trajectory charts. Capital program visualisations showing investment timing and scope. Comparative infrastructure position charts. Executive summary for CEO and Mayor; technical detail in body sections for Directors and Engineers.

## REPORT TYPE 7: FINANCIAL INTELLIGENCE REVIEW

### 1. Old Name (Benchmarking Era)
Financial Performance Report / Financial Benchmarking Summary

### 2. New Name (Intelligence Era)
Financial Intelligence Review

### 3. Description
The Financial Intelligence Review provides comprehensive, analytically interpreted intelligence on an organisation's financial health, sustainability trajectory, and comparative financial position. It moves beyond the financial statements themselves to interpret what the numbers mean in the context of the organisation's specific circumstances, strategic commitments, infrastructure obligations, and community responsibilities. The Review integrates audited financial data, long-term financial planning documents, budget analysis, revenue pattern assessment, expenditure trend analysis, and comparative financial intelligence from similar organisations to produce a coherent financial intelligence picture. It is designed for executive and elected member audiences who need to understand financial health without being financial analysts.

### 4. Primary Audience
CEO, CFO, Finance Director, Mayor and Elected Members, Audit and Risk Committee, State Government Oversight Bodies

### 5. Key Intelligence Dimensions Covered
- Financial sustainability intelligence
- Revenue intelligence (structure, trajectory, sources)
- Expenditure intelligence (patterns, drivers, efficiency signals)
- Capital investment intelligence
- Debt and liability management intelligence
- Long-term financial trajectory
- Comparative financial intelligence
- Financial risk signals

### 6. Suggested Structure and Sections
1. Financial Intelligence Summary
2. Financial Health Overview
3. Revenue Intelligence Analysis
4. Expenditure Intelligence Analysis
5. Capital Investment Intelligence
6. Debt and Liability Intelligence
7. Long-Term Financial Trajectory
8. Comparative Financial Intelligence
9. Financial Risk Signals
10. Strategic Financial Considerations
11. Intelligence Sources

### 7. Recommended Cadence
Annual financial intelligence review aligned with audit completion. Mid-year financial intelligence update for budget review contexts. Triggered review for significant financial events (major capital decisions, financial difficulty signals, rating agency assessments).

### 8. Visual Treatment Notes
Clean, data-rich design with strong chart quality. Revenue and expenditure trend charts spanning minimum five years. Comparative financial position visualisations. Financial trajectory projections with assumptions clearly stated. Plain language interpretation of financial ratios and sustainability indicators. Not designed to look like an audit report.

## REPORT TYPE 8: STRATEGIC FORESIGHT REPORT

### 1. Old Name (Benchmarking Era)
Strategic Planning Data Pack / Future Trends Summary

### 2. New Name (Intelligence Era)
Strategic Foresight Report

### 3. Description
The Strategic Foresight Report provides forward-looking intelligence designed to support long-term strategic planning, risk management, and opportunity identification. It synthesises emerging trends, developing signals, demographic trajectories, economic forecasts, policy directions, technological changes, and environmental dynamics into a structured intelligence picture of what is likely to matter for an organisation and its community over the next three to ten years. The Report draws on the full depth of the AIIPD Place Intelligence Database — including longitudinal patterns, early-signal identification, and comparative intelligence from jurisdictions that may be further along relevant trajectories — to support the kind of evidence-based, forward-looking strategic thinking that genuinely improves long-term governance and planning.

### 4. Primary Audience
CEO, Executive Leadership Team, Strategic Planning Teams, Mayor and Elected Members, State Government Partners

### 5. Key Intelligence Dimensions Covered
- Demographic trajectory intelligence
- Economic and employment foresight
- Community need and aspiration trajectories
- Infrastructure demand forecasting
- Environmental and climate foresight
- Policy and regulatory direction intelligence
- Technology and service delivery foresight
- Governance environment signals
- Financial sustainability foresight

### 6. Suggested Structure and Sections
1. Strategic Foresight Summary
2. Strategic Context: The Next Decade
3. Demographic Foresight Intelligence
4. Economic and Employment Foresight
5. Community and Social Foresight
6. Infrastructure Demand Foresight
7. Environmental and Climate Foresight
8. Policy and Regulatory Environment Signals
9. Technology and Service Delivery Signals
10. Emerging Strategic Risks
11. Emerging Strategic Opportunities
12. Strategic Intelligence Implications
13. Intelligence Sources and Foresight Methodology

### 7. Recommended Cadence
Produced for each strategic planning cycle (typically every four years). Annual strategic foresight update for high-change environments. Triggered for significant external events that materially affect organisational strategy.

### 8. Visual Treatment Notes
Forward-looking visual design language — trajectory charts, scenario visualisations, horizon maps. Avoid presenting projections as certainties. Signal and trajectory distinctions clearly communicated. Strategic implication narratives prominent alongside evidence. Executive-focused summary designed for strategic planning workshop use.

## REPORT TYPE 9: EMERGING RISK INTELLIGENCE BRIEF

### 1. Old Name (Benchmarking Era)
Risk Summary / Risk Register Update

### 2. New Name (Intelligence Era)
Emerging Risk Intelligence Brief

### 3. Description
The Emerging Risk Intelligence Brief is AIIPD's focused, timely intelligence product for risk-relevant conditions developing in an organisation's environment. Unlike a traditional risk register — which catalogues known and accepted risks — the Emerging Risk Brief identifies signals that have not yet crystallised into defined risks but warrant strategic attention. It draws on continuous intelligence integration, pattern recognition across comparable organisations, and DRMC-powered signal analysis to surface developing conditions — community tensions, financial stress signals, infrastructure deterioration patterns, governance environment changes, policy shifts — before they require emergency response. The Brief is intentionally concise and action-oriented, designed for rapid executive consumption and strategic response planning.

### 4. Primary Audience
CEO, Executive Leadership Team, Risk and Audit Committee, Mayor and Elected Members (selected issues)

### 5. Key Intelligence Dimensions Covered
- Community and social risk signals
- Financial risk signals
- Governance and political risk intelligence
- Infrastructure risk signals
- Environmental and climate risk intelligence
- Policy and regulatory risk signals
- Workforce and organisational risk intelligence
- Reputational risk signals

### 6. Suggested Structure and Sections
1. Risk Intelligence Summary (one page, executive focused)
2. Emerging Risk Signals: High Attention
3. Emerging Risk Signals: Monitor
4. Contextual Risk Intelligence (comparative patterns)
5. Early Intervention Opportunities
6. Intelligence Sources

### 7. Recommended Cadence
Quarterly emerging risk intelligence brief. Event-triggered brief for significant environmental changes. Annual comprehensive risk intelligence review.

### 8. Visual Treatment Notes
Concise, action-oriented design. One-page executive summary is the primary deliverable for senior audiences. Signal categorisation by domain and attention level. Evidence links visible for senior advisors who want to go deeper. Designed for distribution in a leadership team meeting without extensive reading time required.

Regional Intelligence Synthesis

The Synthesis is designed for audiences making decisions that affect multiple local governments — regional planning, infrastructure investment, service coordination, policy design, and economic development — providing the regional intelligence architecture that those decisions require.

## REPORT TYPE 11: EXECUTIVE INTELLIGENCE SUMMARY

### 1. Old Name (Benchmarking Era)
Executive Summary Report / Management Dashboard Report

### 2. New Name (Intelligence Era)
Executive Intelligence Summary

### 3. Description
The Executive Intelligence Summary is AIIPD's high-level, fast-consumption intelligence product designed specifically for senior executive and elected member audiences who need the most important intelligence in the most accessible format. It distils the full intelligence picture — across all relevant dimensions — into a concise, narrative-led, visually clear summary that can be read in 10 to 15 minutes. The Summary does not sacrifice analytical rigour for brevity — every statement is evidence-grounded and source-attributable — but it presents intelligence in a format calibrated for non-technical, time-pressured audiences making consequential decisions. It is designed to be used in CEO briefings, mayoral briefings, leadership team meetings, and strategic planning workshops.

### 4. Primary Audience
CEO, Mayor and Elected Members, Executive Leadership Team, Board or Advisory Committee

### 5. Key Intelligence Dimensions Covered
- Priority intelligence signals (current)
- Organisational intelligence overview
- Strategic intelligence summary
- Emerging risk signals
- Emerging opportunity signals
- Comparative intelligence context (brief)

### 6. Suggested Structure and Sections
1. Intelligence Highlights (half page — three to five key intelligence items)
2. Organisation at a Glance (one page — current intelligence snapshot)
3. Intelligence Signals: What Requires Attention
4. Intelligence Signals: What Represents Opportunity
5. Strategic Context Summary
6. Intelligence Implications: Suggested Conversation Points for Leadership

### 7. Recommended Cadence
Quarterly executive intelligence summary. Event-triggered summaries for significant developments. Annual comprehensive executive intelligence review.

### 8. Visual Treatment Notes
Premium document design. Maximum five pages including cover. Heavy use of intelligence callouts and highlight boxes. Minimal data tables — distil to charts and narratives. Designed to be printed and brought to a meeting, or read on a device in a 15-minute window.

## REPORT TYPE 12: ANNUAL INTELLIGENCE REVIEW

### 1. Old Name (Benchmarking Era)
Annual Performance Review / Annual Benchmarking Report

### 2. New Name (Intelligence Era)
Annual Intelligence Review

### 3. Description
The Annual Intelligence Review is AIIPD's comprehensive, longitudinal intelligence product produced once per year to provide a complete intelligence picture of an organisation's year — integrating all intelligence dimensions, tracking progress against strategic commitments, assessing changes in the organisational and community context, and identifying the intelligence signals that should shape the year ahead. It serves as both a strategic accountability document and a forward planning intelligence resource — providing the full evidence picture that leadership teams, governing bodies, and accountability partners need for informed assessment and strategic direction-setting. The Annual Review is designed to be the primary intelligence document for annual general meetings, strategic planning sessions, and year-end governance reviews.

### 4. Primary Audience
CEO, Full Executive Leadership Team, Mayor and Elected Council, State Government Partners, Community (summary version)

### 5. Key Intelligence Dimensions Covered
- All intelligence dimensions (comprehensive coverage)
- Year-on-year intelligence trajectory
- Strategic plan implementation intelligence
- Financial intelligence review
- Community intelligence update
- Governance intelligence assessment
- Infrastructure intelligence update
- Comparative intelligence context
- Emerging risk and opportunity signals for the year ahead

### 6. Suggested Structure and Sections
1. Annual Intelligence Summary (3 pages)
2. Strategic Context: The Year in Review
3. Governance Intelligence Review
4. Financial Intelligence Review
5. Community Intelligence Update
6. Infrastructure Intelligence Update
7. Workforce Intelligence Update
8. Environmental Intelligence Update
9. Strategic Plan Implementation Intelligence
10. Comparative Place Intelligence Context
11. Intelligence Trajectory: Year-on-Year Analysis
12. Emerging Signals for the Year Ahead
13. Intelligence for Strategic Planning
14. Complete Intelligence Sources

### 7. Recommended Cadence
Annual production aligned with financial year. Produced after annual report publication and audit completion.

### 8. Visual Treatment Notes
Flagship document design — the most visually substantial intelligence product in the AIIPD suite. Cover page with year, organisation name, and place imagery. Comprehensive data visualisations including longitudinal trend charts spanning full organisational history. Annual comparison tables and trajectory graphics. Full intelligence taxonomy visible in the document structure. Designed for formal presentation at council and executive level.
