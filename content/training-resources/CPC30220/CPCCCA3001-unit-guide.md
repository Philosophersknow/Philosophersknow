## What This Unit Is About

Openings are where buildings leak, stick, rattle, and fail inspection. A door that binds, a window that admits water, or a lintel that sags are all traceable to the framing and fitting stage — and all of them are expensive to correct once linings and finishes are on.

CPCCCA3001 asks you to frame openings correctly, install door and window units plumb, level, square and weathertight, and fit the associated hardware.

---

## Opening Terminology

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| **Rough opening** | The framed opening, sized larger than the unit to allow for packing and adjustment |
| **Jamb stud** | Full-height stud at each side of the opening |
| **Trimmer stud** | Shortened stud fixed inside the jamb stud, supporting the lintel |
| **Lintel / header** | Member spanning the opening, carrying the load above |
| **Jack stud / cripple** | Short stud between lintel and top plate, or between sill and bottom plate |
| **Sill trimmer** | Horizontal member forming the bottom of a window opening |
| **Reveal** | The lining of the opening between the frame and the internal wall face |
| **Head, jamb, sill** | Top, sides and bottom members of the door or window unit itself |
| **Packer / shim** | Material inserted between the unit and the frame to adjust position |
| **Flashing** | Impervious material directing water out of the wall |
| **Sill tray / pan flashing** | Flashing under a window sill, turned up at the ends |
| **Margin** | The visible gap between a door leaf and its frame |

## Rough Opening Size

**The single most common error in this unit is framing the opening to the finished unit size.**

The rough opening must be **larger** than the unit to allow for:
- The frame thickness
- Packing on both sides and at the head
- Adjustment for plumb and square
- Building movement

**Always obtain the manufacturer's specified rough opening dimensions.** They vary between products and between manufacturers. Do not calculate them from the door leaf size or assume a standard allowance.

**Typical allowance guidance** — but confirm against the specific product:

| Element | Typical allowance over unit size |
|---|---|
| Width | 10–20mm total (5–10mm each side) |
| Height | 10–20mm |

**[VERIFY: Rough opening allowances against the specific manufacturer's installation instructions for the product being fitted. These are not standardised across the industry.]**

**Standard Australian door leaf sizes** (nominal, in mm):

| Height | Common widths |
|---|---|
| 2040 | 620, 720, 770, 820, 870, 920 |
| 2340 | 720, 820, 870, 920 |

A 820mm leaf is the most common internal door width and is the minimum for compliant accessible access in many applications.

## Lintels

The lintel carries the load above the opening and transfers it to the jamb and trimmer studs.

### Sizing

Lintel size is determined by:
- **Span** of the opening
- **Load** carried — roof only, roof and ceiling, or roof, ceiling and upper floor
- **Roof load width (RLW)** or floor load width, taken from the plans
- **Timber stress grade** — MGP10, MGP12, F17, LVL
- **Wind classification** for uplift considerations

**These are read from AS 1684 span tables**, or specified by an engineer on the structural drawings.

**Never guess a lintel size.** An undersized lintel deflects, causing doors to bind and windows to jam, and in extreme cases fails. It will also fail frame inspection.

**Where the structural drawings and the architectural drawings disagree on a lintel, stop and escalate.** Structural drawings generally take precedence, but the conflict must be resolved by the builder or engineer, not by the carpenter on site. See CPCCCM1012.

### Support

- Lintels bear on **trimmer studs** at each end, fixed inside the jamb studs
- The bearing length must meet the specified minimum
- Fixing and tie-down per AS 1684 for the wind classification

## Framing the Opening — Sequence

1. **Confirm the rough opening size** from the manufacturer's specification, not from the leaf or sash size
2. **Mark out on the plates** — set out both plates together so studs align top and bottom (see CPCCCA3003)
3. **Install jamb studs** — full height, plumb
4. **Install trimmer studs** inside the jamb studs, cut to the underside of the lintel
5. **Install the lintel** on the trimmers, correctly sized and fixed
6. **Install jack studs** from lintel to top plate at the standard stud spacing
7. **For windows: install the sill trimmer** at the correct height, with jack studs below to the bottom plate
8. **Check the opening** — diagonals equal, jambs plumb, head level, opening square and to size

**Check before the unit arrives, not after.** An opening 15mm out of square is a five-minute fix at framing stage and a major problem once the wall is clad.

## Installing Window Units

### Before Installation

- **Check the unit against the schedule** — correct size, correct opening type, correct handing, correct glazing
- **Inspect for transit damage** — cracked glass, distorted frames, damaged finishes
- **Check the opening** is square, plumb and to size
- **Confirm the sill height** against the plans

### Weatherproofing — The Critical Sequence

Water management around openings is where buildings fail. The governing principle is that **water must always be directed outward and downward, and every layer must lap over the layer below it.**

**Correct sequence for a typical framed wall:**

1. **Sill flashing / pan flashing first** — installed into the opening before the window, turned up at both ends and at the back to form a pan. If water gets past the window, this catches it and directs it out
2. **Wall wrap** lapped correctly — the wrap above the opening laps **over** the head flashing; the wrap below laps **under** the sill flashing
3. **Window installed** and fixed
4. **Head flashing** over the window head, extending past the jambs, with stop ends
5. **Jamb flashings** where specified
6. **Sealant** applied where specified — **at the head and jambs, but the sill is typically left unsealed** so that any water entering can drain out

**Sealing the sill traps water inside the wall.** This is a common and damaging error — it converts a drainable assembly into a reservoir.

**[VERIFY: Flashing and weatherproofing details against the manufacturer's installation instructions, AS 4773 / AS 2047 as applicable, and the NCC. Requirements vary by wall system, cladding type, and exposure.]**

### Fixing

- **Pack at the fixing points** before fixing — packing supports the frame so it is not distorted when fixed
- Fix through the packers, not into an unsupported span of frame
- Fix at the spacings specified by the manufacturer
- **Do not overtighten** — this bows the frame and can cause sashes to bind or glass to stress

### Checking

- **Plumb both jambs**
- **Level the head and sill**
- **Square** — measure diagonals
- **Wind** (twist) — sight across the frame; a twisted frame will not seal
- **Operate the sashes** — smooth movement, even margins, correct latching

## Installing Door Units

### Door Types

| Type | Description |
|---|---|
| **Hollow core** | Lightweight, framed core with skins; internal use |
| **Solid core** | Heavier, better acoustic and fire performance |
| **Fire door** | Certified assembly with a fire resistance level (FRL); **must be installed exactly per certification** |
| **External / entry** | Weather-resistant, often solid or purpose-built |
| **Sliding / cavity slider** | Runs on a track; cavity units require framing during wall construction |
| **Bi-fold** | Multiple leaves hinged together |

**Fire doors are a certified system.** The door, frame, hardware, seals and installation method are certified together. **Substituting any component or deviating from the installation instructions voids the certification.** Do not cut, plane, or modify a fire door.

### Fitting a Door Leaf

1. **Check the frame** is plumb, square, and not twisted. **Fix the frame first** — a door cannot be fitted accurately to a frame that is out
2. **Check the leaf** for the correct size and for any bow or twist
3. **Determine the margins.** Typical margins: approximately **2–3mm at the head and sides**, and a bottom clearance appropriate to the floor finish — larger where carpet is to be laid, or where an air gap is required for ventilation
4. **Mark and plane** the leaf to achieve even margins. Plane the hinge stile first, then the lock stile
5. **Ease the leading edge** of the lock stile slightly (a shallow bevel) so the door does not bind on the frame as it closes
6. **Mark hinge positions** — commonly 150–200mm from the top and bottom of the leaf, with a third hinge centred or slightly above centre on heavier or external doors
7. **Cut hinge housings (gains)** to the exact hinge leaf thickness. **Too deep and the door binds on the hinge side; too shallow and the door will not close and the margins open up on the lock side**
8. **Hang the door**, check the swing and margins, adjust
9. **Fit hardware** — latch, lock, handles, strike plate

### Handing

Door handing describes which way a door is hinged and which way it swings. Conventions vary, so **always confirm the convention in use** before ordering or fitting hardware.

A common Australian convention describes handing viewed **from the outside** (or from the side the door opens away from), identifying whether the hinges are on the left or right.

**[VERIFY: Handing convention with the supplier and the project documentation. Getting handing wrong means the wrong hardware and a return.]**

### Hardware

- **Latch and lock** — mortice or tubular; bore centres and backset must match the hardware
- **Backset** — the distance from the edge of the door to the centre of the handle spindle; commonly 60mm or 70mm
- **Strike plate** — housed into the frame so it is flush; the latch must engage cleanly without forcing
- **Door stops** — prevent the handle damaging the wall
- **Seals** — acoustic, smoke, or weather seals as specified

## Common Faults and Their Causes

| Fault | Cause |
|---|---|
| **Door binds on hinge side** | Hinge gains cut too deep |
| **Door will not latch; gap opens on lock side** | Hinge gains too shallow, or frame not plumb |
| **Door swings open or closed on its own** | Frame out of plumb — the door is finding its lowest point |
| **Uneven margins** | Frame out of square, or leaf not planed accurately |
| **Door catches at the top corner** | Frame twisted, or leaf bowed |
| **Window sash binds** | Frame fixed out of square, overtightened fixings, or twisted frame |
| **Water entering at window** | Sill flashing omitted or sealed shut; flashing laps reversed; head flashing without stop ends |
| **Sagging lintel; doors and windows jamming after completion** | Undersized lintel |

**Almost every one of these is a framing or fixing error, not a product fault.** The instinct to blame the door is usually wrong.

## Worked Example

**Prompt:**

*You have hung an internal 820 × 2040 hollow core door. When you test it, the door will not latch — there is a visible gap of about 4mm between the leaf and the frame on the lock side at the top, closing to almost nothing at the bottom. The door also swings slowly open by itself when left ajar.*

*Diagnose the problem and describe how you would correct it.*

---

**Exemplary Response:**

Two symptoms are present and they point to the same underlying cause. I would not start adjusting the door until I had checked the frame.

---

**What the symptoms tell me:**

**Symptom 1 — the gap tapers.** The margin on the lock side is 4mm at the top and near zero at the bottom. That is a **tapering margin**, which means the leaf and the frame are not parallel. Either the leaf has been planed out of square, or the frame is out of plumb.

**Symptom 2 — the door swings open on its own.** This is the more informative symptom. A door that moves by itself when left ajar is doing so under gravity — it is finding its lowest point. **A correctly hung door on a plumb frame stays where it is put.**

Together these strongly indicate **the frame is out of plumb on the hinge side**, leaning so the door swings away from the latch.

---

**Diagnosis sequence:**

**1. Check the frame before touching the door.**

- **Plumb the hinge jamb** in both directions — across the opening and in the plane of the wall. This is the critical check
- **Plumb the lock jamb**
- **Level the head**
- **Check the frame for twist** — sight across from corner to corner, or use a string line. A frame can be plumb on both jambs and still be twisted out of a single plane, which produces exactly this kind of taper
- **Check the frame is square** — measure diagonals
- **Check the frame width** at head, middle and sill — a frame that has been fixed with the jambs pinched or spread will produce a tapering margin even when plumb

**2. Check the leaf.**

- Is the leaf itself square? Measure the diagonals of the door
- Is the leaf bowed or twisted? Sight along it
- Are the hinge gains consistent in depth?

**3. Check the hinges.**

- Are the gains cut to the correct depth — equal to the hinge leaf thickness?
- Are the screws fully seated? A proud screw head holds the hinge off the timber and changes the door position
- Are the hinges aligned with each other vertically? Misaligned hinges bind and distort

---

**Most likely cause on this evidence:**

The hinge jamb is **out of plumb**, leaning back so that the top of the door falls away from the latch and the door swings open under gravity.

This is consistent with both symptoms simultaneously, which is why I would look here first. A leaf planed out of square would produce the tapering margin, but would **not** cause the door to swing open by itself.

---

**Correction:**

**If the frame is out of plumb — the correct fix:**

The frame must be repacked and refixed. This is more work than adjusting the door, but adjusting the door to fit an out-of-plumb frame is a false economy — the margins can be made to look acceptable, but the door will still swing on its own, the latch will be under strain, and the fault will be visible to anyone who checks with a level.

- Remove or back off the fixings on the hinge jamb
- Re-plumb the jamb using a long level, checking in both planes
- **Insert packers behind the jamb at each fixing point** — a frame fixed without packing at the fixing point will pull out of plumb as the screws are tightened
- Refix, then **re-check plumb after fixing**, not before. Fixing changes the position
- Re-check the head is level and the frame is not twisted
- Rehang and test

**If the frame is plumb and the leaf is the problem:**

- Re-plane the lock stile to achieve an even margin, taking the taper out
- Maintain the slight bevel on the leading edge so the door does not bind on closing
- Re-check the margin at head, middle and bottom

**If the hinge gains are inconsistent:**

- Gain too deep on one hinge pulls the door toward the hinge side at that point. Pack behind the hinge leaf with card or thin packer to bring it out
- Gain too shallow holds the door off the frame. Deepen the gain to the hinge leaf thickness

---

**After correcting:**

- Margins even at head and both sides — approximately 2–3mm
- Bottom clearance appropriate to the floor finish
- Door stays where it is put when left ajar
- Latch engages cleanly without forcing, and the strike plate does not need to be over-adjusted to compensate
- Door does not bind at any point through its swing

---

**The judgement point:**

The temptation here is to move the strike plate to catch the latch and call it done. That would make the door latch, but it would not fix anything — the door would still swing on its own, the margin would still taper, and the strike would be pulling the door out of its natural position every time it closed.

**The symptoms are telling me the frame is wrong. Fixing the frame fixes both symptoms; fixing the strike fixes neither.**

**Examiner Annotation:** Outstanding. The student reads both symptoms together rather than treating them separately, and correctly identifies that the self-swinging door is the diagnostic key — it points to frame plumb in a way the tapering margin alone does not. The check sequence is correct in order, starting with the frame before the leaf, and includes checks that candidates commonly omit: frame twist, frame width at three points, and proud screw heads holding a hinge off the timber. The correction correctly identifies packing at fixing points as essential and — importantly — notes that plumb must be re-checked *after* fixing, since tightening changes the position. The closing rejection of the strike plate shortcut names the exact wrong answer this scenario is designed to elicit and explains why it fails. This is diagnostic reasoning, not procedure recall. Strong Competent.
