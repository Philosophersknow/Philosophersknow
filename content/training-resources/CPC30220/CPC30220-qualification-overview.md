# CPC30220 — Certificate III in Carpentry
## Qualification Overview and Student Resource Guide

**Training Package:** CPC — Construction, Plumbing and Services Training Package
**Qualification Code:** CPC30220
**AQF Level:** Certificate III
**Total Units:** 27 (21 core + 6 elective)
**Typical Duration:** 3–4 years (apprenticeship); shorter for recognition pathways
**Delivery Mode:** Apprenticeship (on-the-job + off-the-job training at a TAFE or RTO)

---

## What This Qualification Is

The Certificate III in Carpentry is the trade qualification for carpenters in Australia. It is completed as an apprenticeship — combining structured learning at a registered training organisation (RTO) or TAFE with on-the-job training under a qualified tradesperson.

Carpentry is one of the largest construction trade categories in Australia. Carpenters work across residential, commercial, and civil construction — framing, roofing, formwork, fitting, fixing, and finishing.

## The Construction Industry Context

### Industry Structure
The Australian construction industry is one of the largest employing sectors in the country. It includes:
- **Residential construction:** Houses, townhouses, apartments
- **Commercial construction:** Offices, retail, hospitality, health, education facilities
- **Civil construction:** Infrastructure — roads, bridges, tunnels (more relevant to CPC30120 and RII)
- **Industrial construction:** Factories, warehouses, processing facilities

### Trade Licensing
Unlike some professions, the trades are licensed at the state/territory level. A Certificate III in Carpentry is a trade qualification — but building work above a certain value typically requires a builder's licence (usually a Certificate IV in Building and Construction — CPC40120 — or higher). The carpentry trade qualification qualifies the worker to perform carpentry work; a builder's licence is required to contract for and manage building projects.

**[VERIFY: Licensing thresholds and requirements vary significantly by state. Always check with your state's building licensing authority.]**

### Work Health and Safety in Construction
Construction is a high-risk industry. WHS in construction is covered both by general WHS legislation and by specific construction WHS regulations in each state. The most visible safety requirement is the **General Construction Induction** (White Card — CPCCWHS1001) which must be completed before working on a construction site.

## How You Will Be Assessed

Construction trade assessment involves both knowledge assessment and practical/performance assessment:

| Method | What It Tests |
|---|---|
| Written knowledge questions | Plans and specifications, materials, WHS legislation, calculations |
| Practical observation | Performing the trade task to industry standard on site or in a simulated environment |
| Third-party report | Employer/supervisor evidence of on-the-job performance |
| Portfolio evidence | Documentation of tasks completed in the workplace |
| Logbook | Record of tasks and skills demonstrated over the apprenticeship |

**The critical difference from classroom qualifications:** In a trade apprenticeship, **practical demonstration is the primary evidence**. Written knowledge supports it. You cannot pass a carpentry unit without demonstrating the physical skill to industry standard.

## The White Card — CPCCWHS1001

Before beginning work on any construction site in Australia, you must hold a current General Construction Induction Card — commonly called the **White Card**. This is completed as a separate short course (not part of CPC30220 but typically a prerequisite or co-requisite for site work).

The White Card covers:
- Hazard identification and risk management on construction sites
- Workplace rights and responsibilities
- WHS legislation and the construction industry
- Emergency procedures

White Cards are issued by state WHS regulators and recognised nationally.

## Key Trade Knowledge Areas

### Timber and Materials
- Species and grades of structural timber
- Engineered wood products (LVL, I-beams, glulam)
- Building materials: sheet materials, fixing systems, insulation, moisture barriers
- Selecting appropriate materials for the application and environment

### Plans and Specifications
- Reading and interpreting working drawings
- Understanding scales, symbols, and conventions
- Site plans, floor plans, elevations, sections, details
- Building specifications and their relationship to drawings

### Calculations
- Calculating materials quantities (timber, sheet materials, fixings)
- Length, area, and volume calculations
- Pitch and run calculations for roofing
- Safe work load calculations for elevated work platforms

### Building Code
- Understanding the National Construction Code (NCC) / Building Code of Australia (BCA) in relation to carpentry work
- Tolerances and standards required for compliant work
- Energy efficiency requirements affecting framing and cladding

## Sustainability in Construction

CPC30220 includes sustainability requirements through CPCCCM1012. Key sustainability concepts for carpenters:

- **Material efficiency:** Minimising offcuts and waste; ordering to minimise waste
- **Recycled and sustainable timber:** FSC certification, sustainably sourced timber
- **Thermal performance:** Correct installation of insulation for energy efficiency
- **Waste management:** Correct sorting and disposal of construction waste
- **Water-sensitive design:** Correct flashing and waterproofing to prevent moisture ingress

## Apprenticeship Structure

A carpentry apprenticeship is typically a 4-year training contract (though this varies by state and can be shortened through recognition of prior learning). The structure is:

- **On-the-job:** Working with an employer (building company, carpentry subcontractor) under a qualified carpenter
- **Off-the-job:** Block release, day release, or online training at a TAFE or RTO
- **Assessment:** Combination of on-site observation, knowledge tests, and third-party evidence

The apprentice is employed throughout the training period and receives a training wage (set by the relevant modern award — the Construction, Forestry, Maritime, Mining and Energy Union [CFMEU] Award or equivalent).
