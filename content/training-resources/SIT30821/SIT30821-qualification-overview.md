## What This Qualification Is

The Certificate III in Commercial Cookery is the foundational trade qualification for chefs and cooks in Australia. It covers the skills required to prepare and present food commercially — in restaurants, hotels, catering, cafés, aged care facilities, schools, and any other commercial kitchen environment.

This qualification can be completed as a traineeship (while employed in a kitchen) or as a full-time program at a TAFE or private RTO. Many programs include mandatory industry placement.

## The Hospitality Industry in Australia

### Industry Scale
Hospitality is one of Australia's largest industries. It encompasses:
- **Restaurants and cafés:** From fine dining to fast casual
- **Hotels and resorts:** Food and beverage operations within accommodation venues
- **Catering:** Events, institutions (hospitals, schools, aged care), airlines, mining camps
- **Pubs, bars, and clubs**
- **Quick service (QSR):** Fast food and food court operations

### Employment Conditions
The **Restaurant Industry Award 2020** (or relevant modern award) governs employment conditions for hospitality workers in most commercial kitchens. Key provisions:
- Minimum wage rates by classification (Kitchen Hand, Cook Level 1, Cook Level 2, Chef de Partie, etc.)
- Penalty rates for evenings, weekends, and public holidays
- Leave entitlements under the National Employment Standards
- Junior rates (for workers under 21)

### Industry Culture
Commercial kitchens have a distinctive culture — high pressure, physically demanding, fast-paced, team-dependent. Understanding kitchen hierarchy and culture is part of operating effectively in this environment:
- **Head Chef / Executive Chef:** Overall kitchen leadership, menu development, staff management
- **Sous Chef:** Second in command; often leads service
- **Chef de Partie (CDP):** Responsible for a specific section (entrée, main, pastry, grill)
- **Commis Chef / Cook:** Junior level; works within a section under CDP or Sous Chef
- **Kitchen Hand:** Non-cooking support — washing, cleaning, food prep assistance

## Food Safety — The Non-Negotiable Foundation

Food safety is not an add-on in commercial cookery — it is the foundation on which all food preparation rests. Every cook must understand:

### Food Safety Legislation
Each state and territory has food safety legislation based on the **Australia New Zealand Food Standards Code** and the national **Food Safety Standards** (Food Standards Australia New Zealand — FSANZ):

- **Standard 3.1.1:** Interpretation and Application
- **Standard 3.2.1:** Food Safety Programs (for food service businesses)
- **Standard 3.2.2:** Food Safety Practices and General Requirements
- **Standard 3.2.2A:** Food Safety Management Statements
- **Standard 3.2.3:** Food Premises and Equipment

### Temperature Control — The Core of Food Safety
The **Temperature Danger Zone** is 5°C to 60°C — the range in which food-borne pathogens (bacteria that cause illness) multiply rapidly.

**Key temperature rules:**
- Hot food must be kept at 60°C or above
- Cold food must be kept at 5°C or below
- Cooked food must reach an internal temperature of at least 75°C (core temperature)
- The 2/4 hour rule: food kept in the danger zone for 0–2 hours can be used immediately; 2–4 hours must be used immediately or discarded; more than 4 hours — discard

### HACCP (Hazard Analysis and Critical Control Points)
HACCP is the internationally recognised food safety management system. It identifies:
- **Biological hazards:** Bacteria, viruses, parasites
- **Chemical hazards:** Cleaning agents, pesticides, food additives in excess
- **Physical hazards:** Foreign objects — metal, glass, bone, plastic

A HACCP plan identifies **Critical Control Points (CCPs)** — stages in food preparation where controls are essential to prevent food safety hazards. In a commercial kitchen, CCPs typically include receiving, storage, thawing, cooking, cooling, and reheating.

## Kitchen Equipment Safety

Commercial kitchens contain high-risk equipment. Key safety requirements:

- **Gas appliances:** Understand pilot light procedures; report gas leaks immediately; never leave gas appliances unlit
- **Deep fryers:** Hot oil is a serious burn and fire risk; correct oil temperature monitoring; correct disposal of used oil
- **Sharp knives:** Correct grip, cutting technique, storage, and transport
- **Slicers and other powered cutting equipment:** Safety guards in place; never bypass safety mechanisms
- **Manual handling:** Heavy pots, trays, and boxes — correct lifting technique; use trolleys for heavy loads
- **Burns and scalds:** PPE (aprons, oven mitts), careful handling of hot equipment, communication when moving hot materials ("behind you — hot pan")
