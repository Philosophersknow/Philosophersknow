## Why This Is a Note Rather Than a Unit Guide

This unit is not given a separate full guide, deliberately. Two reasons.

### 1. It substantially duplicates RIIWHS201E

RIIOHS201A uses **OHS** terminology — Occupational Health and Safety — which is the framework that preceded the harmonised **WHS** (Work Health and Safety) legislation adopted in most Australian jurisdictions from 2012.

The competency content is materially the same as **[RIIWHS201E — Work safely and follow WHS policies and procedures](RIIWHS201E-unit-guide.md)**: identifying hazards, following site safety systems and documentation, applying controls, using PPE, reporting incidents, and understanding worker duties and rights.

Writing a second guide covering the same competency in superseded terminology would produce a document that:
- Repeats existing content
- Uses terminology that no longer matches current legislation, site documentation, or the language a student will encounter on site
- Risks teaching a student to sit an assessment using terms their workplace has not used for over a decade

### 2. Its inclusion in current packaging requires verification

Two options, in order of preference:

**Option A — Contextualise RIIWHS201E.** Add a short terminology mapping section to the existing RIIWHS201E guide, covering the OHS-to-WHS equivalences, and cross-reference it for RIIOHS201A. This avoids duplication and gives the student the current framework with the historical terminology mapped onto it.

**Option B — Write a separate guide.** Only if the assessment requirements differ materially, rather than only in terminology.

**Recommendation: Option A.** The competency is the same; only the vocabulary differs.

## Terminology Mapping — OHS to WHS

Provided so that a student encountering either set of terms can work between them.

| OHS term | WHS equivalent | Note |
|---|---|---|
| **Occupational Health and Safety (OHS)** | **Work Health and Safety (WHS)** | The harmonised framework broadened "occupational" to "work" to capture people beyond employees |
| **Employer** | **PCBU** — Person Conducting a Business or Undertaking | PCBU is deliberately broader: it captures sole traders, partnerships, and principal contractors, not only employers |
| **Employee** | **Worker** | Broader again — includes contractors, subcontractors, labour hire, apprentices, trainees, work experience students and volunteers |
| **Duty of care** | **Primary duty of care** | Similar concept; the WHS Act articulates it more specifically |
| **So far as is practicable** | **So far as is reasonably practicable** | The WHS formulation includes the five specified matters to be weighed, with cost considered last |
| **OHS representative** | **Health and Safety Representative (HSR)** | HSRs under WHS have specific statutory powers, including issuing provisional improvement notices and directing unsafe work to cease |
| **OHS committee** | **Health and Safety Committee** | |
| **Hazard identification and risk assessment** | Unchanged | |
| **Hierarchy of control** | Unchanged | |
| **Incident notification** | **Notifiable incident** | The WHS Regulations define notifiable incidents specifically |

**The substantive changes under WHS, beyond terminology:**

- **Broader duty holders.** Duties extend to officers, designers, manufacturers, importers, suppliers and installers, not only employers
- **Officer due diligence.** Officers of a PCBU have a positive, personal duty to exercise due diligence
- **Broader definition of worker**, so duties are owed to contractors and labour hire personnel on the same basis as employees
- **Consultation obligations** are more explicit, including consultation between duty holders sharing a workplace
- **Right to cease unsafe work** and protection from discriminatory conduct are expressly provided

**[VERIFY: WHS harmonisation has not been adopted uniformly. Western Australia and Victoria have followed different paths and timelines. Confirm which framework and which Act applies in the jurisdiction where the student is studying and working.]**

## For Students

**If your RTO has enrolled you in RIIOHS201A**, work from the **[RIIWHS201E guide](RIIWHS201E-unit-guide.md)** and use the mapping table above to translate the terminology.

**In an assessment, use the terminology your RTO's assessment tool uses.** If the assessment says OHS, answer in OHS terms. If it says WHS, answer in WHS terms. The concepts are the same and an assessor is marking the competency, not the vocabulary — but matching the language of the question is straightforwardly easier to mark.

**On site, use WHS terminology.** It is what current legislation, site documentation, SWMS, permits and inductions use.

---

## For RTOs

If you deliver RIIOHS201A, this note identifies a currency question worth resolving in your own validation: whether the unit remains appropriate to deliver, and whether your assessment tools use terminology consistent with the legislation currently applying in your jurisdiction.

An assessment tool referring to "employers" and "employees" and "so far as is practicable" is assessing against a framework that has been superseded in most of Australia. That is a validation finding waiting to be made.
