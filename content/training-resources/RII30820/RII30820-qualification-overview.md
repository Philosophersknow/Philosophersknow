## What This Qualification Is

The Certificate III in Civil Construction provides the skills and knowledge for workers in civil construction — roads, earthworks, drainage, utilities infrastructure, and civil maintenance. Civil construction underpins all other built infrastructure: without civil construction workers, roads do not exist, water cannot be reticulated, and buildings have no foundations.

Workers with this qualification typically operate plant, perform civil construction tasks, and contribute to the construction and maintenance of public infrastructure.

## The Civil Construction Industry

### What Civil Construction Covers
- **Road construction and maintenance:** Preparation of subgrades, base courses, bitumen sealing, concrete paving
- **Earthworks:** Cut and fill operations, compaction, embankment construction
- **Drainage:** Stormwater systems, culverts, channels, pipe installation
- **Utilities:** Trenching and installation of water mains, sewers, gas pipelines, and electrical conduit
- **Bridges and structures:** Formwork, reinforcement, concrete placement for bridges and retaining walls
- **Mining and resources infrastructure:** Haul roads, processing facility infrastructure

### Who Employs Civil Construction Workers
- Civil construction companies (tier 1, 2, and 3 contractors)
- Local councils — roads and drainage maintenance
- State and federal government infrastructure agencies
- Mining and resources companies
- Utilities organisations (water, power, gas)

## WHS in Civil Construction

Civil construction is a high-risk industry. Key hazards include:

| Hazard | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Plant and vehicles** | Heavy mobile plant — excavators, graders, rollers, trucks — creates crush, run-over, and collision risks; exclusion zones are essential |
| **Underground services** | Excavation near underground power, gas, water, and communications services; "Dial Before You Dig" mandatory before any excavation |
| **Excavation collapse** | Unsupported excavation walls can collapse without warning — shoring, battering, or exclusion zones required |
| **Traffic management** | Working on or adjacent to live traffic requires traffic management plans and traffic controllers |
| **Falls** | Working in trenches, on embankments, or on structures |
| **Silica dust** | Cutting, grinding, or breaking concrete, brick, and sandstone releases crystalline silica — engineering controls (wet cutting, extraction) and P2 respirators required |
| **Noise** | Heavy plant, compaction equipment, and concrete cutting — hearing protection required |
| **Vibration** | Whole-body vibration from plant; hand-arm vibration from compactors and jack hammers — can cause chronic musculoskeletal conditions |

### Dial Before You Dig
Before any excavation in Australia, contractors are legally required to contact Dial Before You Dig (1100 or dialbeforeyoudig.com.au) to obtain information on the location of underground services. Striking an underground power cable or gas main can be fatal.

### Traffic Management
Working on or adjacent to a road requires a Traffic Management Plan (TMP) and accredited traffic controllers. The traffic management requirements come from:
- State road authority standards
- AS 1742.3 — Manual of Uniform Traffic Control Devices (MUTCD) Part 3: Traffic Control Devices for Works on Roads

## Civil Construction Technical Foundations

### Materials Testing
Civil construction requires ongoing testing of materials to confirm they meet specification:

| Test | What It Measures |
|---|---|
| **Compaction test (modified Proctor / field density test)** | Whether compacted soil or granular material meets the specified density requirement |
| **CBR (California Bearing Ratio)** | Strength of subgrade or base course material — used to design pavement thickness |
| **Slump test (concrete)** | Consistency/workability of fresh concrete |
| **Aggregate grading** | Particle size distribution of granular materials |
| **Moisture content** | Water content of soil — critical for compaction |

### Plans and Specifications
Civil construction workers must be able to read:
- **Site plans:** Location, boundaries, existing features
- **Alignment drawings:** Road centreline, horizontal curves
- **Cross-sections:** Cut/fill profile at specified chainage
- **Longitudinal sections:** Vertical profile of the road or drain along its length
- **Detail drawings:** Culvert, kerb, drainage structures
- **Specifications:** Materials, compaction standards, tolerances

## Environmental Obligations in Civil Construction

Civil construction has significant environmental impact. Key obligations:

- **Erosion and sediment control:** Disturbed soil must be stabilised or contained to prevent sediment entering waterways (silt fences, sediment basins, revegetation)
- **Concrete washout:** Concrete washwater is highly alkaline — must be contained and not discharged to waterways or stormwater
- **Fuel and chemical spills:** Refuelling and chemical handling must include spill containment; spills must be reported and remediated
- **Heritage and cultural sites:** Excavation must stop if artefacts or human remains are discovered; authorities must be notified
- **Noise and vibration:** Construction hours may be regulated by local council or the project environmental management plan
