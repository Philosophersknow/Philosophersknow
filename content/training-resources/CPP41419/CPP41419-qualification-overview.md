## What This Qualification Is

The Certificate IV in Real Estate Practice is the foundational qualification for real estate agents and property managers across Australia. In most states and territories, this qualification (or its predecessor) is a mandatory requirement to obtain a real estate licence.

**Important state variation:** Real estate is licensed at the state/territory level. Licensing requirements — including which qualification is required, what additional work experience is needed, and what CPD is mandated — vary by jurisdiction. Always check with your state licensing authority before commencing this qualification.

## The Central Challenge of Real Estate Assessment

Real estate sits at the intersection of **commercial performance** and **legal/ethical obligation**. The assessments in this qualification consistently test whether you understand that:

1. Your obligation to your client (vendor/landlord) does not override your obligations to buyers and tenants under consumer protection and property legislation
2. Your commercial interest (earning commission) must never compromise your legal duties or ethical obligations
3. Every transaction involves a web of documentation requirements, disclosure obligations, and trust account rules that must be followed precisely

Students who approach real estate assessment purely as a sales skills exercise typically fail units like CPPREP4002 (ethics) and CPPREP4003 (legislation). The assessor is checking whether you understand the *legal framework within which real estate operates* — not just whether you can close a sale.

## Key Concepts — Know Before You Start

### Agency Law
A real estate agent is an agent who acts on behalf of a principal (the vendor or landlord). Agency law governs the relationship:
- The agent has a *fiduciary duty* to the principal — highest duty of loyalty, honesty, and good faith
- The agent must avoid conflicts of interest
- The agent must disclose material facts to the principal
- The agent cannot profit from the relationship beyond the agreed commission without disclosure

### Trust Accounting
Deposits, rent, and other funds received on behalf of clients must be held in a separate trust account — not the agency's operating account. Trust accounting legislation is strict; breaches can result in licence cancellation and criminal charges.

### Cooling-off periods
Most residential property contracts in Australia include a cooling-off period during which the buyer can rescind the contract (with possible penalty). The conditions, duration, and exclusions vary by state.

### Material disclosure
Agents have an obligation to disclose *material facts* — information that a reasonable buyer or tenant would consider relevant to their decision. This includes known defects, pending legal proceedings, flood or bushfire risk, and other matters. Failure to disclose material facts can expose agents and vendors to legal action.
