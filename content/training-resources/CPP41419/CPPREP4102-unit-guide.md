## What This Unit Is About

Marketing a property for sale is not just putting it on the internet. It is a strategic process of presenting the property to the right buyers through the right channels, at the right price, with the right messaging — in a way that creates competition and maximises the vendor's outcome. CPPREP4102 asks you to demonstrate that you understand the full marketing process, from creating a marketing plan to preparing legal advertising materials.

---

## Elements of Competency

### Element 1: Establish marketing requirements

You need to:
- Understand the vendor's goals and timeline for the sale
- Develop a marketing strategy matched to the property type, location, and buyer profile
- Explain marketing options and costs clearly to the vendor
- Obtain vendor approval for all marketing materials before use

### Element 2: Prepare marketing materials

You need to:
- Prepare written marketing copy that is accurate, compliant, and compelling
- Understand the legal requirements for advertising real property
- Prepare or coordinate photography, floor plans, and other marketing collateral
- Ensure all advertising complies with consumer protection legislation (ACL)

### Element 3: Implement the marketing campaign

You need to:
- List the property on appropriate platforms
- Coordinate advertising schedules
- Manage and respond to buyer enquiries generated by the campaign
- Track marketing performance against targets

### Element 4: Evaluate and adjust the campaign

You need to:
- Monitor enquiry levels, inspection numbers, and feedback
- Identify when a campaign is not generating expected results
- Advise the vendor of marketing performance and recommended adjustments
- Document marketing activities and outcomes

## The Marketing Plan

A marketing plan for a residential property typically includes:

### Buyer Profile
Who is most likely to buy this property? Young families, downsizers, investors, first home buyers? The buyer profile determines which channels and messaging will be most effective.

### Method of Sale
Private treaty, auction, expressions of interest — the method affects the marketing strategy. Auctions typically require a defined campaign period (4–6 weeks) with a clear end date. Private treaty campaigns can run longer.

### Marketing Channels

| Channel | Description | Best For |
|---|---|---|
| **Online portals** | Domain, realestate.com.au | All properties — primary channel for buyer search |
| **Agency website** | Organisation's own property listings | Brand and existing buyer database |
| **Social media** | Facebook, Instagram, targeted ads | Reaching local buyers; lifestyle properties; sharing with buyer networks |
| **Print advertising** | Local newspapers, property guides | Some markets — less dominant than 10 years ago |
| **Email to database** | Agent's own buyer database | Matching known buyers; off-market; first-to-know campaigns |
| **Signage** | For-sale board at the property | Local awareness; passing traffic; signals activity in the area |
| **Property styling** | Professional staging | Maximises presentation; particularly valuable for vacant properties |
| **Photography and video** | Professional photos, virtual tours | Essential for online listings |
| **Floor plans** | Dimensioned plans | Buyers assess layout; important for filtering self-qualified buyers |

### Marketing Budget
Vendors are typically asked to contribute to marketing costs (advertising fees, photography, signage). In some agencies, these are a condition of listing; in others, they are presented as options. **Key point:** The vendor's money must be managed carefully. Advertising funds received from vendors must be managed according to the agency agreement and trust accounting rules.

## Legal Requirements for Real Estate Advertising

### Australian Consumer Law
All advertising must comply with the ACL:
- Must not be misleading or deceptive
- Must not make false representations about the property
- Must not misrepresent the price

### Underquoting
Underquoting — advertising or indicating to buyers a price that is below the agent's estimate or the vendor's minimum — is illegal in most Australian states. It wastes buyers' time and creates false market signals.

**NSW:** Agents must record an estimated selling price and cannot advertise below it. The "Statement of Information" (SOI) must be provided to buyers showing 3 comparable sales.

**VIC:** Agents must provide a "Statement of Information" containing 3 comparable sales and a selling price range. The lower bound cannot be more than 10% below the estimated price.

**[VERIFY: Current underquoting rules vary by state. Check current Fair Trading/Consumer Affairs guidance.]**

### Advertising a Price
Many vendors want to advertise a specific price or price range. This must reflect the vendor's genuine asking price and the agent's genuine market assessment — not a tactical low figure to attract interest.

### Photography and Description Standards
Property photos must accurately represent the property. Digital manipulation that materially misrepresents the property (e.g., making a room appear larger than it is, removing a power line from a photo) can constitute misleading conduct.

Written descriptions must not include:
- False claims about the property's features
- Unsubstantiated superlatives (claims like "best in the street" should be capable of justification)
- Incorrect area or boundary information

## Writing Effective Marketing Copy

Effective real estate copy:
- Leads with the most compelling selling point
- Uses specific, verifiable language (not just "stunning" and "gorgeous")
- Addresses the target buyer's priorities
- Includes key details buyers need (bedrooms, bathrooms, parking, land size)
- Has a clear call to action (inspections, contact details)

**Example — weak copy:**
"Stunning family home in a great location. Features a gorgeous kitchen and beautiful gardens. Don't miss this one!"

**Example — stronger copy:**
"A rare north-facing double-block in [Suburb], this 4-bedroom family home offers 650sqm of level land and a newly renovated kitchen with stone benchtops and 900mm freestanding oven. Within the [School] catchment and 400m to the train station — a lifestyle opportunity that rarely comes to market in this pocket."

## Worked Example

**Prompt:**

*You have listed a 4-bedroom house in an inner suburb. After 3 weeks on the market (private treaty at $1,050,000), you have had only 4 enquiries and 2 inspections. Properties in comparable locations are selling in an average of 28 days. What do you do, and what do you tell the vendor?*

---

**Exemplary Response:**

Four enquiries and two inspections in three weeks is below expectation for a private treaty in this market. I need to diagnose the cause before recommending action.

**Possible causes to investigate:**
1. **Price:** Is $1,050,000 above comparable market evidence? I would review recent comparable sales — have any new sales occurred in the last 3 weeks that affect the analysis?
2. **Presentation:** Are the photos performing? How many views are we getting on the portal listings? A high view count with low enquiry suggests interest but something is deterring contact (typically price). A low view count suggests the listing is not appearing in the right searches.
3. **Marketing channels:** Have we maximised our channel reach? Are we appearing on both major portals? Has the vendor agreed to social media advertising?
4. **Listing quality:** Is the copy engaging? Does the first photo lead strongly?

**What I tell the vendor:**

I would contact the vendor promptly — they have a right to know how the campaign is performing and what I am observing. I would present:
- The enquiry and inspection numbers vs. what would be expected
- Portal views and enquiry rates (if available from the portal dashboard)
- Any feedback from the two inspections
- My assessment of whether price is the likely factor or whether other elements of the campaign need adjustment

If price appears to be the issue — particularly if portal views are high but enquiry is low, or if comparable properties are selling below our asking price — I have a professional obligation to raise a price review with the vendor. This conversation needs to be evidence-based: "We have had these comparable sales occur since we listed. The market evidence suggests the property is more likely to achieve [range]."

If the vendor is unwilling to adjust the price, I document their decision. My obligation is to give them my honest professional advice and document it; the decision is ultimately theirs.

**Examiner Annotation:** This response correctly diagnoses rather than assuming one cause, presents the analysis to the vendor with evidence, correctly identifies the agent's obligation to raise a price review if warranted, and correctly notes that the final decision belongs to the vendor. The student does not suggest inflating marketing spend as a substitute for addressing price. Strong Competent.
