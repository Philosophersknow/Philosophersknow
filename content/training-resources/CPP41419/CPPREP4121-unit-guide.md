## What This Unit Is About

Property management is a distinct professional practice within real estate, governed by its own legislation, requiring its own skills, and carrying its own ethical obligations. While sales agents focus on transacting property, property managers focus on managing ongoing relationships — with landlords (property owners), with tenants, and with the regulatory framework that governs both.

CPPREP4121 focuses on establishing and maintaining the relationships that are central to property management: understanding the landlord's needs and obligations, setting up the tenancy correctly, and managing the ongoing relationship with both landlord and tenant professionally and lawfully.

---

## The Property Manager's Role

A property manager acts as the agent of the landlord (the "owner" or "lessor") to manage a rental property. This creates a fiduciary relationship — the property manager's primary duty is to the landlord.

However — and this is critical — property managers also have legal obligations to tenants that cannot be set aside by the landlord's instructions. A landlord cannot instruct a property manager to do something that breaches the Residential Tenancies Act or anti-discrimination legislation.

**Core property management responsibilities:**
- Finding and qualifying suitable tenants
- Managing the application and selection process (lawfully)
- Establishing the tenancy (lease, bond, entry condition report)
- Collecting and accounting for rent
- Managing maintenance and repairs
- Conducting routine inspections
- Handling rent arrears and tenancy breaches
- Managing tenancy renewals and endings
- Accounting to the landlord (monthly statements, trust accounting)

---

## The Property Management Agreement

Like a sales agency agreement, a property management agreement is the legal authority for the agent to act on behalf of the landlord. Key elements:

- **Property address**
- **Term of the management agreement**
- **Management fees** (percentage of rent collected; flat fee; or combination)
- **Letting fee** (typically charged when a new tenant is placed — often equivalent to 1–2 weeks' rent)
- **Disbursement authority** (what the agent can spend on repairs without prior landlord approval — e.g., up to $500)
- **Advertising authority** (consent to advertise the property)
- **Rent review authority** (can the agent negotiate rent increases or must the landlord approve each one?)

## Qualifying and Selecting Tenants

The tenant selection process must comply with anti-discrimination legislation. Landlords cannot — and property managers cannot act on instructions to — discriminate against applicants based on:
- Race, ethnicity, or national origin
- Sex, sexual orientation, or gender identity
- Age
- Disability
- Religion
- Family status (having children) — protected in most states
- Source of income (e.g., receiving Centrelink payments) — protected in some states

**Legitimate screening criteria:**
- Ability to pay rent (rental history, employment income, financial capacity)
- Rental history and references
- Personal references
- Proposed occupants and their suitability for the property

**Assessment process:**
Applications should be assessed against documented criteria, consistently applied. If you reject an applicant, you should be able to explain the decision in terms of legitimate screening criteria — not protected characteristics.

## Establishing the Tenancy

### Lease Agreement
The Residential Tenancy Agreement (lease) is the legal contract between the landlord and tenant. Standard lease forms are mandated in most states — agents must use the prescribed form (or a form containing all the required information).

**Key lease terms:**
- Property address
- Names of all tenants
- Commencement date
- Rent amount and frequency
- Bond amount
- Term (fixed or periodic)
- Rent review provisions
- Conditions of occupancy

### Bond (Security Deposit)
The bond is a deposit held against the risk of damage, unpaid rent, or breach of the tenancy. Bond amounts are regulated by legislation (typically capped at 4 weeks' rent for residential leases).

**Critical requirement:** The bond must be lodged with the relevant state tenancy authority within the time required by legislation. It cannot be held by the agency or the landlord. [VERIFY: current lodgement timelines by state]

### Entry Condition Report
The entry condition report documents the condition of the property at the start of the tenancy. It is critical — it is the baseline against which any damage claims at the end of the tenancy are assessed.

The entry condition report must be:
- Completed thoroughly (every room, every fixture)
- Accompanied by photographs
- Provided to the tenant for their review and comment
- Returned by the tenant with any disagreements noted
- Retained by the agency for the duration of the tenancy and beyond

## Ongoing Property Management

### Routine Inspections
Regular inspections allow the property manager to:
- Confirm the property is being maintained in accordance with the tenancy agreement
- Identify maintenance issues
- Provide the landlord with confidence in the tenancy

Inspection frequency limits and notice requirements are set by state legislation. Generally: notice must be given (typically 7 days for routine inspections in most states), and inspections cannot be too frequent.

### Rent Collection and Rent Arrears
**Rent collection:** Property managers collect rent on behalf of the landlord and pay it to the landlord (less fees) in accordance with the trust accounting requirements and the management agreement.

**Rent arrears:** When rent is not paid, the property manager must act in accordance with state legislation. Typically:
- A formal breach notice is issued after a specified number of days in arrears
- If the breach is not remedied, an application to the tenancy tribunal can be made
- Eviction is a last resort and can only be effected through the tribunal — not by the landlord or agent directly

**A landlord cannot instruct the agent to cut off utilities, change the locks, or remove a tenant's belongings.** These are illegal means of eviction — they expose the landlord and the agent to significant legal liability.

### Maintenance and Repairs
The landlord has a legal obligation to maintain the property in a habitable condition and to carry out repairs in a timely manner. Property managers must:
- Receive and communicate repair requests to the landlord
- Arrange emergency repairs even without prior landlord approval (in accordance with the disbursement authority and state legislation)
- Document all maintenance requests and responses

## Worked Example

**Prompt:**

*Your landlord, Mr Barker, calls you to say he has decided he wants to terminate the tenancy of his current tenant, Ms Lee. Ms Lee has been in the property for 18 months, has always paid rent on time, and has not caused any problems. Mr Barker says he "just doesn't want her there anymore" and asks you to tell her to leave within the week. What do you tell Mr Barker?*

---

**Exemplary Response:**

I need to tell Mr Barker clearly that this is not legally possible in the way he is describing.

Ms Lee is a lawful tenant who has met all her obligations. She has rights under the Residential Tenancies Act that the landlord — and I — must comply with. Those rights cannot be cancelled because the landlord has changed his mind.

**What the legislation says:**
A landlord can terminate a tenancy at the end of a fixed term by giving the required notice, or during a periodic tenancy by giving the required notice. In most Australian states, this means:
- If Ms Lee is on a periodic tenancy: the landlord must give a minimum notice period (typically 60–90 days depending on the state)
- The notice must be in the prescribed form
- The notice cannot require Ms Lee to vacate in one week

**In some states,** the landlord may also require a valid reason to terminate a periodic tenancy during the term ("no-grounds termination" is being progressively abolished across Australia — check your state's current legislation).

**What I would tell Mr Barker:**
"Mr Barker, I understand you want to end the tenancy. I need to let you know that under the Residential Tenancies Act, Ms Lee has rights as a tenant that we both need to comply with. We cannot ask her to leave within a week. The correct process is to serve a formal notice to vacate, which requires [X weeks' notice] under [state legislation]. I'll prepare that notice in the correct form, and Ms Lee's tenancy will end on [date]. I also need to advise you that in [state], you may need to provide a reason for ending the tenancy — I'll check the current requirements and advise you further."

**What I would NOT do:**
- Tell Ms Lee she has to leave within a week
- Change the locks or take any self-help action to terminate the tenancy
- Simply follow Mr Barker's instruction without explaining his legal obligations

**Examiner Annotation:** This response correctly identifies the tenant's legislative rights, refuses to follow an unlawful instruction, explains the correct process to the landlord professionally, correctly notes the "no grounds" issue as state-specific, and clearly states what is not permissible. The student demonstrates that the agent's fiduciary duty to the landlord does not include facilitating illegal action. Strong Competent.
