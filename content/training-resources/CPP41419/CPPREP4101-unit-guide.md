## What This Unit Is About

A property appraisal is an agent's professional estimate of the price a property is likely to achieve in the current market. It is one of the most important skills in real estate — and one of the most ethically loaded. An accurate, well-researched appraisal builds trust and leads to realistic expectations. An inflated appraisal designed to win a listing (a practice called "price baiting" or "conjunctive selling above market") damages the vendor and eventually the agent's reputation.

CPPREP4101 asks you to demonstrate that you can research market data, apply appraisal methodology, and present a credible and honest appraisal to a vendor or landlord.

---

## Appraisal vs Valuation

This distinction is critical and frequently tested.

| | **Appraisal** | **Valuation** |
|---|---|---|
| Who does it | Licensed real estate agent | Registered property valuer |
| Legal status | Agent's market estimate — not a legal determination of value | Formal legal determination of market value |
| Use | Listing price guidance; marketing strategy | Mortgage lending; legal proceedings; stamp duty; compulsory acquisition |
| Liability | Subject to professional obligations; not as strictly regulated as valuations | Strictly regulated; valuers carry professional indemnity; can be used in court |

**A real estate agent cannot provide a "valuation" — only a valuer can.** If a client needs a formal valuation (for mortgage purposes, a deceased estate, a court matter), you should recommend they engage a registered valuer.

## The Appraisal Process

### Step 1: Research Comparable Sales (Comps)

The foundation of any appraisal is comparable sales — recent sales of similar properties in the same or closely comparable locations. You are looking for:

- Properties similar in type (house, unit, townhouse), size, bedrooms/bathrooms, and features
- Sales in the same suburb or immediate area
- Sales within the last 3–6 months (older sales are less relevant in a moving market)
- Sales conditions — were there any unusual circumstances that affected the price?

**Sources of comparable sales data:**
- Real estate portals (sold listings on Domain, realestate.com.au)
- Titles office/land registry data
- RP Data, CoreLogic, PriceFinder and similar professional subscription services
- Your agency's own sales records

**Adjust for differences:** No two properties are identical. If a comparable sold for $850,000 but had a pool and your subject property does not, you need to adjust. This adjustment is a professional judgement informed by market data.

---

### Step 2: Inspect the Property

An appraisal cannot be completed without inspecting the property. During inspection, note:

- **Location factors:** Street type (main road vs quiet street), proximity to schools, transport, shops, parks, noise
- **Property condition:** State of maintenance, recent renovations, presentation
- **Unique features:** Pool, garage, large land, views, aspect (north-facing preferred in most markets)
- **Negatives:** Nearby development sites, power lines, flood risk, heritage restrictions
- **Land size and dimensions:** Particularly relevant in markets where development potential adds value

---

### Step 3: Analyse Market Conditions

A property's value is also affected by current market conditions:

- **Days on market:** How long are properties taking to sell? A falling days-on-market figure indicates strong demand.
- **Clearance rates (auction markets):** High clearance rates indicate strong buyer competition.
- **Stock levels:** How many properties are currently listed? High stock = more buyer choice = more price pressure on vendors.
- **Interest rate environment:** Rising rates reduce borrowing capacity and typically soften demand. [VERIFY: current rate environment]
- **Seasonal patterns:** Spring typically produces more listings and more buyers in Australian markets.

---

### Step 4: Prepare the Appraisal

Present the appraisal to the vendor with:

1. **Comparable sales evidence** — at least 3–5 recent comparable sales with key details and adjustments explained
2. **Market overview** — brief summary of current market conditions in the area
3. **Your estimated selling range** — the price range the property is likely to achieve, presented as a range rather than a single figure in most cases
4. **Recommended method of sale** — private treaty, auction, expressions of interest (see below)
5. **Recommended marketing strategy** — this connects to CPPREP4102

---

## Methods of Sale

| Method | How It Works | Best Used When |
|---|---|---|
| **Private treaty** | Property listed at a price; negotiation between buyer and vendor via agent | Most common method; suits most residential properties |
| **Auction** | Public bidding process on a set date; sold unconditionally to highest bidder above reserve | Strong demand; properties likely to attract competitive interest; desirable areas |
| **Expressions of Interest (EOI)** | Buyers submit offers by a deadline; vendor selects preferred buyer | Unique properties; commercial; where price guidance is difficult |
| **Tender** | Formal sealed bid process | Commercial; large transactions |

## Rental Appraisal

The same methodology applies to rental appraisals — researching comparable leased properties, inspecting the subject property, considering current rental market conditions, and presenting a recommended weekly rent.

**Key metric: Rental yield**

Gross rental yield = (Annual rent ÷ Property purchase price) × 100

Example: A property purchased for $600,000 achieving $500/week in rent
= ($500 × 52 ÷ $600,000) × 100 = 4.33% gross yield

Net yield accounts for property expenses (management fees, maintenance, rates, insurance).

## Ethical Obligations in Appraisal

**Price baiting / inflated appraisal:** Deliberately overestimating the likely selling price to win a listing, with the intention of "conditioning" the vendor to accept a lower price later, is:
- Misleading conduct under the Australian Consumer Law
- A breach of the agent's duty of honesty to their client
- A complaint trigger and potential grounds for disciplinary action

**Underquoting:** Quoting buyers a price range that is below the vendor's stated minimum (or below the agent's estimate of likely selling price) to generate buyer interest is also misleading conduct and is specifically regulated in several states (particularly NSW and VIC).

**Your obligation:** Give the vendor your honest, evidence-based professional assessment — even if it is lower than what they hope to achieve. A vendor who lists at the right price achieves a better outcome than one who lists too high and waits months with price reductions.

## Worked Example

**Prompt:**

*You have been asked to appraise a 3-bedroom, 1-bathroom house in an inner suburb. You have researched the following comparable sales in the last 90 days:*

*1. 3bed/1bath house, same street — sold $785,000 (required significant renovation)*
*2. 3bed/2bath house, adjacent suburb — sold $920,000 (renovated, larger land)*
*3. 3bed/1bath house, same suburb — sold $835,000 (similar condition, slightly smaller land)*
*4. 3bed/1bath unit (not house) — sold $690,000 (ignore — different property type)*
*5. 3bed/1bath house, same suburb — sold $850,000 (similar condition, same land size)*

*The subject property is in good condition and does not require renovation. What is your appraisal range and how do you justify it?*

---

**Exemplary Response:**

**Comparable analysis:**

- **Comparable 1 ($785,000)** is in the same street — high relevance for location — but required significant renovation. The subject property does not. A significant uplift applies — renovation in this suburb is likely worth $50,000–$80,000. Adjusted estimated value: $835,000–$865,000.

- **Comparable 2 ($920,000)** is in an adjacent suburb and has an extra bathroom and larger land. Both differences justify a premium over the subject. This comparable provides an upper ceiling for aspirational pricing but is not directly comparable. Limited direct relevance.

- **Comparable 3 ($835,000)** is in the same suburb, similar condition, slightly smaller land. The subject has comparable or larger land, which could justify a slight premium. This is highly relevant. Adjusted: $840,000–$855,000.

- **Comparable 4 ($690,000)** is a unit, not a house — not comparable on property type. Excluded.

- **Comparable 5 ($850,000)** is in the same suburb, similar condition, same land size. Highly relevant — essentially a close match. This is the strongest comparable: $850,000.

**Appraisal conclusion:**

Based on the most relevant comparables (Comps 3 and 5, adjusted Comp 1), I would provide the vendor with an appraisal range of **$835,000 – $865,000**, with an estimate that a well-presented and correctly priced property should achieve around $850,000 in the current market.

I would support this with the comparable sales data, explain the adjustments, note current market conditions (days on market, stock levels), and discuss whether private treaty or auction is more appropriate for this property type in this market.

I would not inflate the appraisal to win the listing. If the vendor believes the property should achieve more, I would discuss the evidence with them respectfully — and if they insist on a higher list price, I need to decide whether I can act for them at an unsupported price.

**Examiner Annotation:** This response correctly identifies the most relevant comparables, excludes the non-comparable (unit), applies logical adjustments, arrives at a defensible range, and addresses the ethical obligation not to inflate. Strong Competent.
