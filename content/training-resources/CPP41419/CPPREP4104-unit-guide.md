## What This Unit Is About

A real estate agent's primary legal client is the vendor — but buyers are the people who pay the price that determines the vendor's outcome. Building genuine relationships with buyers is not just good commercial practice; it is required for ethical and effective representation of vendors.

CPPREP4104 asks you to understand how to work with buyers — qualifying their needs and capacity, building trust, conducting inspections professionally, and managing buyer enquiry and communication in a way that is honest and serves both buyer and vendor appropriately.

---

## The Agent's Duty to Buyers

This is one of the most commonly misunderstood aspects of real estate ethics. The agent acts for the vendor — but the agent also has legal obligations to buyers:

- Must not engage in misleading or deceptive conduct (ACL)
- Must disclose material facts to buyers
- Must deal honestly and in good faith
- Must not use high-pressure tactics or unconscionable conduct

**What the agent does NOT owe the buyer:**
- Fiduciary duty (this is owed to the vendor)
- Confidentiality about the vendor's marketing strategy or sales method (the agent can share this)

**What the agent DOES NOT disclose to the buyer:**
- The vendor's minimum acceptable price (reserve or "walk away" price)
- That the vendor is under financial pressure and must sell

These disclosures would breach the agent's fiduciary duty to the vendor.

## Buyer Qualification

Understanding a buyer's situation allows the agent to match the right properties to the right buyers — and to present the vendor with buyers who are genuinely capable of proceeding.

**Key qualifying questions:**

| Question | Why It Matters |
|---|---|
| What type of property are you looking for? | Matches them to appropriate listings |
| What suburbs or areas are you focused on? | Location filter |
| What is your budget? | Critical — an unqualified buyer wastes everyone's time |
| Are you pre-approved for finance? | Indicates seriousness and capacity to proceed |
| Are you also selling? | Timing implications — a buyer who must sell first may not be able to proceed quickly |
| What is your timeline for purchasing? | Urgency; settlement requirements |
| Have you looked at any other properties? | Understanding market awareness and decision stage |

**Finance pre-approval vs conditional approval:**
Pre-approval means a lender has indicated they will lend up to a specified amount, subject to assessment of a specific property. It does not guarantee a loan — it must be confirmed against the specific property the buyer wants to purchase. An unconditional approval means the lender has approved the specific loan — much stronger.

## Conducting Open Inspections

Open inspections (open homes) are scheduled times when any member of the public can inspect the listed property. They are one of the primary ways buyers engage with properties and one of the most public-facing parts of an agent's work.

**Before the inspection:**
- Ensure the property is presented to its best: clean, well-lit, temperature comfortable
- Prepare relevant materials: brochures, contract for sale, disclosure documents
- Know the property: features, inclusions, any known issues

**During the inspection:**
- Register all attendees (name and contact details) — this is both a security and a marketing requirement
- Greet buyers warmly and professionally
- Allow buyers to move through the property without excessive pressure or shadowing
- Be available to answer questions accurately
- Do not misrepresent the property or omit material facts in response to direct questions

**After the inspection:**
- Follow up with enquiries from registered buyers
- Provide feedback to the vendor (aggregate)
- Record any buyer interest for follow-up

## Buyer Communication and Follow-Up

**The importance of follow-up:**
Most buyers do not make an offer at or immediately after the first inspection. Consistent, non-pressuring follow-up keeps the property front of mind.

**Effective follow-up:**
- Contact buyers within 24 hours of an inspection
- Ask open questions: "What were your thoughts on the property?" "What else are you considering?"
- Listen to objections — they often indicate what the buyer needs to feel comfortable proceeding
- Match buyers to any new listings that suit their profile

**Managing buyer expectations:**
Buyers sometimes have unrealistic expectations about price or what they can purchase within their budget. Part of building a genuine relationship with buyers is providing an honest picture of the market — what $800,000 buys in this area, why a property they like is priced where it is.

This serves the vendor too: a buyer who understands market reality is more likely to make a competitive offer.

## Confidentiality Obligations Regarding Buyers

While the agent's primary duty is to the vendor, the agent does collect personal information about buyers (contact details, financial position, circumstances). This information:

- Must be handled in accordance with the Privacy Act 1988 (Cth)
- Should not be disclosed to other buyers without consent
- Should not be used for purposes other than the property transaction

---

## Buyers' Agents

A buyers' agent (or buyer's advocate) is an agent who acts exclusively for buyers in property transactions. Their role is the mirror image of the listing agent:
- They research properties on behalf of the buyer
- They conduct due diligence
- They negotiate on the buyer's behalf
- Their fiduciary duty is to the buyer, not the vendor

When a buyer is represented by a buyers' agent, the listing agent must be aware that they are communicating with a professional representative — and must still disclose material facts, but should not disclose vendor confidential information to the buyers' agent any more than to the buyer directly.

## Worked Example

**Prompt:**

*You are conducting an open inspection. A buyer approaches you and says: "I love this property. Can you tell me what the vendor will actually take — what's their bottom line? I don't want to waste everyone's time." How do you respond?*

---

**Exemplary Response:**

This is a very common question and one that requires a clear, confident, and honest response.

I cannot tell the buyer the vendor's bottom line — doing so would be a direct breach of my fiduciary duty to the vendor. The vendor has entrusted me to represent their interests, and their minimum acceptable price is confidential information. Disclosing it would undermine the vendor's negotiating position and potentially cost them money.

What I would say to the buyer:

"I appreciate you being direct — it saves us both time. I'm not able to share the vendor's specific expectations because that information is confidential to them. What I can tell you is that the vendor is genuine about selling and the property is priced based on current comparable sales in the area. If you're interested, the best approach is to put in an offer that reflects what the property is worth to you — and if we're close enough, we'll find a way to make it work."

This response:
- Declines to disclose confidential information without being rude or making the buyer feel interrogated
- Confirms the vendor is genuine (which is helpful information for a serious buyer)
- Invites the buyer to make an offer
- Does not misrepresent my position or the vendor's flexibility

If the buyer pushes further and says "I've heard this place has been on the market for months — the vendor must be desperate" — I would respond honestly but carefully: "I'm not going to characterise the vendor's position, but the property is generating good interest and we expect to see offers soon."

**Examiner Annotation:** This response correctly identifies the obligation (cannot disclose) and explains why (fiduciary duty to vendor), then pivots constructively to invite an offer. The language is professional and does not alienate the buyer. The student handles the "vendor is desperate" follow-up correctly — not denying or confirming, not fabricating interest.
