## What This Unit Is About

Before you can act as a real estate agent, you need to understand the structure of the industry — how it is licensed, what the roles are, how agencies operate, and what professional standards apply. CPPREP4001 establishes the foundation. It asks you to understand where real estate fits in the broader property sector, how the profession is regulated, and what being a professional in this industry actually requires.

## The Real Estate Industry — Structure and Roles

### Categories of Real Estate Practice

**Residential sales:** The sale of houses, units, and other residential properties on behalf of vendors. The most visible form of real estate work.

**Property management:** Managing residential (and commercial) properties on behalf of landlords — leasing, rent collection, maintenance coordination, tenancy management.

**Commercial real estate:** Sales and leasing of commercial, industrial, and retail properties. Higher value transactions with different legal and market dynamics.

**Business broking:** The sale of businesses (as distinct from property). Requires specific licensing or knowledge in some states.

**Buyers' agents:** Acting exclusively for buyers in property acquisitions — a growing sector distinct from vendor agency.

**Rural and regional:** Sales and management of rural properties, farms, and associated assets.

---

### Agency Roles

| Role | Responsibilities | Licensing Requirement |
|---|---|---|
| **Principal licensee / Licensee in charge (LIC)** | Responsible for the agency; accountable for all statutory obligations including trust accounts | Full real estate licence (higher tier) |
| **Licensed agent** | Can conduct real estate transactions independently | Real estate licence |
| **Certificate holder / Registration** | Can conduct real estate work under supervision of a licensed agent | Certificate IV (CPP41419) in most states |
| **Property manager** | Manages tenancies on behalf of landlords; may have separate licensing requirements in some states | Varies by state |

**[VERIFY: Exact role terminology and licensing tiers vary significantly by state. NSW uses "licensee in charge," "agent," and "certificate holder." VIC uses "licensed estate agent" and "agent's representative." Always verify the current licensing structure in your state.]**

## Licensing Requirements

Real estate is licensed at the state/territory level. There is no single national real estate licence. However, CPP41419 is the foundational qualification required in most jurisdictions.

**General process to become a licensed real estate agent:**
1. Complete CPP41419 Certificate IV in Real Estate Practice
2. Work under a licensed agent for a required period (varies — typically 1–2 years)
3. Apply to the state licensing authority for a licence
4. Pass any additional requirements (fit and proper person check, criminal history check, financial solvency check)
5. Maintain licence through continuing professional development (CPD)

**State licensing authorities:**

| State/Territory | Licensing Authority | Key Legislation |
|---|---|---|
| NSW | NSW Fair Trading | Property and Stock Agents Act 2002 |
| VIC | Consumer Affairs Victoria | Estate Agents Act 1980 |
| QLD | Office of Fair Trading | Property Occupations Act 2014 |
| SA | Consumer and Business Services | Land Agents Act 1994 |
| WA | DMIRS | Real Estate and Business Agents Act 1978 |
| ACT | Access Canberra | Agents Act 2003 |
| NT | Attorney-General and Justice | Agents Licensing Act 1979 |
| TAS | CBOS | Property Agents and Land Transactions Act 2016 |

## Professional Standards and Codes of Conduct

### Real Estate Institute of Australia (REIA)
The national industry body for real estate. State real estate institutes (REINSW, REIV, REIQ, etc.) are affiliated bodies. Membership provides access to professional resources, advocacy, and a code of ethics.

### Codes of Ethics and Conduct
Most state real estate institutes have a code of ethics binding on members. These codes address:
- Honesty and transparency in dealings
- Avoidance of conflicts of interest
- Fair dealing with clients, customers, and colleagues
- Professional presentation and conduct

**Important:** Membership of a professional association is voluntary. The legally binding standards come from legislation and licensing conditions — not just from professional body codes.

## The Agency Agreement — Foundation of the Client Relationship

A real estate agent can only act for a vendor or landlord under a valid **agency agreement** (also called an appointment to act, listing agreement, or management agreement depending on the context).

**Key elements of a valid agency agreement:**
- Identity of the principal (vendor/landlord)
- Identity of the agent and agency
- The property being listed or managed
- The term of the agreement (how long it runs)
- Commission and fees (clearly stated — a legal requirement in most states)
- Exclusivity (exclusive or non-exclusive agency)
- Authorised actions (what the agent can do — e.g., set a reserve at auction, accept offers)
- Signatures of all parties

**An agent who takes action on a property without a valid agency agreement has no authority to act and no entitlement to commission.**

## Professional Obligations in Practice

### Continuing Professional Development (CPD)
Licensed agents are required to complete CPD to maintain their licence. CPD requirements vary by state and typically cover ethics, legislation updates, and practice skills.

### Record-keeping
Real estate agents must keep records of all transactions, trust account dealings, and client communications in accordance with state legislation. Records are typically required to be kept for 5–7 years.

### Trust Accounts
Any money received on behalf of clients (deposits, rent) must be held in a separately maintained trust account, not the agency's operating account. Trust accounting is strictly regulated and breaches can result in licence cancellation and criminal charges.

### Fit and Proper Person Requirements
Licence applicants and holders must meet a "fit and proper person" standard. This typically excludes people who have:
- Been convicted of dishonesty offences
- Been declared bankrupt
- Had a previous licence cancelled for misconduct

## Worked Example

**Prompt:**

*Jordan has completed CPP41419 and wants to work as a licensed real estate agent in Queensland. Jordan has been offered a role at an agency. Describe the steps Jordan needs to take to become authorised to conduct real estate transactions in Queensland, and identify any ongoing obligations once licensed.*

---

**Exemplary Response:**

Jordan has completed the foundational qualification — CPP41419 Certificate IV in Real Estate Practice — which is the required qualification for registration in Queensland. The steps to progress to a full licence are:

**Step 1 — Registration as a Salesperson**
In Queensland, the initial credential after completing CPP41419 is registration as a real estate salesperson with the Office of Fair Trading (OFT). Jordan can work under the supervision of a licensed agent at this level. Registration requires:
- Proof of completing CPP41419
- Fit and proper person declaration (no relevant criminal history, not bankrupt, not having had a previous licence cancelled)
- Payment of the registration fee

**Step 2 — Working under supervision**
Jordan works as a registered salesperson under a licensed agent. In Queensland, to progress to a full agent's licence, Jordan will typically need to accumulate a period of experience (check current OFT requirements).

**Step 3 — Applying for a Full Agent's Licence**
After meeting the experience requirement, Jordan can apply to the OFT for a real estate agent's licence. This allows Jordan to operate independently and potentially manage their own agency.

**Ongoing obligations once licensed:**
- **CPD:** Queensland requires licensed agents to complete ongoing CPD — check REIQ and OFT for current CPD requirements
- **Licence renewal:** Licences must be renewed (annually or on the term set); renewal requires CPD evidence and fee payment
- **Fit and proper person:** Jordan must remain fit and proper — any conviction for dishonesty, bankruptcy, or licence breach in another state must be disclosed
- **Trust account compliance:** If Jordan establishes or manages an agency, strict trust accounting obligations apply
- **Professional conduct:** Jordan is subject to the Property Occupations Act 2014 (Qld) and any codes of conduct applying to their licence category

**[VERIFY: Exact registration and licensing steps, experience periods, and CPD hours with the Office of Fair Trading Queensland — requirements can change and current published requirements should always be checked.]**

**Examiner Annotation:** This response correctly identifies the two-stage process (registration → licence), the role of the OFT, ongoing CPD and renewal obligations, the trust account obligation, and includes an appropriate caveat to verify current requirements. Strong Competent.
