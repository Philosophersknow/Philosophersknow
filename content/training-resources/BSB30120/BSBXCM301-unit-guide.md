## What This Unit Is About

Workplace communication is the foundation of every business role. BSBXCM301 is not just about being able to write emails or answer the phone — it is about understanding how to communicate professionally, appropriately, and effectively across a range of workplace contexts and with a range of audiences.

Assessors want to see that you can adapt how you communicate depending on **who** you are communicating with, **what** the purpose is, and **what format** is appropriate. Professional communication is a skill, not just a personality trait.

---

## Elements of Competency

### Element 1: Identify communication requirements

You need to:
- Understand the purpose of the communication (inform, request, confirm, report, persuade)
- Identify the audience and their communication needs (language, accessibility, formality level)
- Select the appropriate communication channel (email, face-to-face, phone, written report, formal letter)
- Confirm what information needs to be conveyed, what is confidential, and what the expected outcome is

**Key principle:** Communication choice is not random. Email vs. phone vs. face-to-face vs. formal letter each have appropriate use cases. Your assessor wants to see that you can justify *why* you chose a particular channel.

---

### Element 2: Communicate in the workplace

You need to:
- Communicate clearly, concisely, and professionally in writing and verbally
- Use appropriate structure and format for each communication type
- Use language and tone appropriate to the audience and purpose
- Follow organisational communication protocols (templates, sign-off procedures, escalation pathways)
- Practice active listening in verbal communication

---

### Element 3: Receive and process workplace communications

You need to:
- Accurately receive and record messages, instructions, and information
- Respond to incoming communications within required timeframes
- Prioritise communications and escalate urgent matters appropriately
- Store and retrieve communications in line with organisational systems

---

### Element 4: Address communication challenges

You need to:
- Identify barriers to effective communication (language, literacy, hearing, cultural differences)
- Take practical steps to address communication barriers
- Seek clarification when communication is unclear
- Report persistent communication problems to the appropriate person

## Communication Channels — When to Use Each

| Channel | Use When | Avoid When |
|---|---|---|
| **Email** | Formal communication; record required; not urgent; multiple recipients | Complex emotional matters; urgent safety issues; confidential verbal discussions |
| **Face-to-face** | Complex or sensitive matters; relationship building; team discussion; immediate feedback needed | Large-scale distribution needed; formal record required from the outset |
| **Phone** | Urgent; requires back-and-forth; personal touch needed; when email would be too slow | Complex technical information that needs to be read; when a record is essential |
| **Formal letter** | External formal communication; legal or contractual matters; complaints; references | Routine internal communication |
| **Report** | Structured analysis of information; recommendations; project updates; audit findings | Quick updates or simple messages |
| **Meeting minutes** | Official record of decisions and actions from a meeting | General information sharing |
| **Instant message / Teams / Slack** | Quick internal queries; real-time team coordination | Sensitive or confidential matters; anything that needs a formal record |

## Writing Professional Emails — Structure and Standards

Every professional email should have:

**Subject line:** Specific and informative. NOT "Hi" or "Question." YES: "Meeting request — Budget review, Thursday 15 June."

**Salutation:** Appropriate to relationship and formality.
- External / formal: "Dear Ms Clancy," / "Dear Mr Ahmed,"
- Internal / established relationship: "Hi Sarah," / "Good morning James,"
- Unknown recipient: "Dear Sir/Madam," or "To whom it may concern,"

**Opening:** State the purpose clearly in the first sentence. Do not bury the point.

**Body:** One idea per paragraph. Short paragraphs. Use dot points for lists. No walls of text.

**Action/close:** Be explicit about what you are asking the recipient to do, or what you will do next, and by when.

**Sign-off:**
- Formal: "Yours sincerely," / "Kind regards,"
- Standard professional: "Best regards," / "Regards,"
- Internal: "Thanks," (acceptable for brief internal)

**Signature block:** Full name, job title, organisation, phone, email.

## Writing Professional Reports — Structure

A short workplace report typically includes:

1. **Title/header**: Report title, author, date, recipient
2. **Purpose/introduction**: What this report is about and why it was produced
3. **Findings / information**: Organised logically with headings and subheadings
4. **Conclusion**: Summary of key points
5. **Recommendations** (if required): Specific, actionable suggestions
6. **Attachments** (if applicable)

## Active Listening — What It Means in Practice

Active listening is not just waiting for your turn to speak. It means:
- Giving the speaker your full attention (no phone, no laptop)
- Using non-verbal signals (nodding, eye contact, open body language)
- Paraphrasing to confirm understanding ("So what you're saying is...?")
- Asking clarifying questions ("Can you tell me more about...?")
- Not interrupting
- Summarising at the end of the conversation

In an assessment context, you may be required to demonstrate active listening in a role play or describe how you applied it in a scenario. The assessor wants to see specific behaviours, not "I listened carefully."

## Common Mistakes in Communication Assessment Tasks

**1. Informal language in formal tasks**

"Hey, just wanted to check in about the thing we discussed." — NYC for a formal email.

Assessors check: Subject line specificity; correct salutation; professional language throughout; appropriate sign-off.

---

**2. Missing the action**

Writing a long email that never clearly states what the recipient needs to do is one of the most common failures. Every workplace communication should have a clear purpose and, where applicable, a clear call to action or next step.

---

**3. Privacy breaches in scenarios**

If a scenario involves client or employee information, assessors check whether you include that information in contexts where it should not appear (e.g., copying an email to the wrong person, leaving a message on an unverified voicemail with personal details, discussing a colleague's performance in an open-plan office).

---

**4. Report structure errors**

The most common: writing a report as if it were an essay (no headings, flowing paragraphs with no clear structure). Business reports use headings, subheadings, and bullet points to make information scannable.

## Worked Example — Professional Email Task

**Prompt:**

*You are an administration officer at Coastal Property Group. Your manager, Sarah Chen, has asked you to email the IT department to request that a new laptop be set up for a new staff member, Jordan Lee, who starts on Monday 23 June. Jordan will need Microsoft Office, access to the company's property management software (PropertyPro), and an email account. Your manager needs confirmation by Thursday 20 June. Draft the email.*

---

**Exemplary Response:**

**Subject:** New staff IT setup request — Jordan Lee, start date 23 June

Dear IT Support Team,

I am writing to request the setup of a new laptop and system access for an incoming staff member.

**Staff member details:**
- Name: Jordan Lee
- Position: [To be confirmed — please email me if you need this detail]
- Start date: Monday 23 June

**Setup required:**
- Microsoft Office (full suite)
- PropertyPro access (property management software)
- Company email account

Could you please confirm by **Thursday 20 June** that the setup will be completed in time for Jordan's start? If you need any further information, please do not hesitate to contact me.

Thank you for your assistance.

Kind regards,
[Your name]
Administration Officer
Coastal Property Group
T: [phone]
E: [email]

**Examiner Annotation:** Excellent. The subject line is specific and includes the relevant name and date. The opening states the purpose immediately. Key information is presented in a structured, scannable format using a list. The deadline is bolded for visibility. The close invites follow-up and includes a full signature block. Professional language throughout. This email would be ready to send in a real workplace.

---

**Insufficient Response:**

Hi IT,

Can you please set up a new laptop for Jordan who is starting on Monday. They need office and the property software and an email. Our manager wants it done by Thursday.

Thanks

**Examiner Annotation:** NYC. No subject line. Inappropriate salutation for a formal internal request. Vague — which Office suite? What is the property software? What is the start date? "Our manager" — who is the manager? Who is the sender? No deadline specificity. No signature block. This communication would result in follow-up requests and would not meet workplace professional standards. Resubmit with appropriate structure, specific information, professional language, and signature block.
