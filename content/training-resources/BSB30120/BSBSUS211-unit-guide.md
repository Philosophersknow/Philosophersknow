## What This Unit Is About

Sustainability in the workplace is not a separate concern from doing your job well — it is increasingly embedded in how organisations operate, how they meet regulatory requirements, and how they maintain their social licence. BSBSUS211 asks you to understand the environmental and sustainability context of business operations and to demonstrate that you can participate in sustainable work practices at your level.

This unit does not require you to be an environmental specialist. It asks you to understand why sustainability matters in a business context, know what your organisation's sustainability policies and procedures require of you, and take practical steps to reduce environmental impact in your daily work.

---

## Key Concepts

### Sustainability
Sustainability in a business context refers to operating in ways that meet current needs without compromising the ability of future generations to meet their own needs. It covers three dimensions, often called the **triple bottom line**:

- **Environmental:** Minimising pollution, waste, and resource consumption; protecting natural systems
- **Social:** Fair treatment of workers, positive community impact, equitable access
- **Economic:** Long-term viability — a business that degrades its environmental or social conditions is undermining its own long-term economic sustainability

### Corporate Social Responsibility (CSR)
CSR refers to a company's commitment to operate ethically and contribute positively to the community and environment beyond legal minimum requirements.

### Circular Economy
A model in which materials and products are kept in use for as long as possible, with waste and resource input minimised. Opposed to the traditional linear model: take → make → dispose.

## Why Sustainability Matters in Business

### Regulatory and compliance reasons
Australian businesses face increasing environmental regulation:
- National Greenhouse and Energy Reporting (NGER) scheme — reporting obligations for large emitters
- State-based environmental protection legislation
- Building standards (energy efficiency)
- Single-use plastics bans (most states and territories)

### Commercial reasons
- Consumer demand: many consumers and B2B clients now factor sustainability into purchasing decisions
- Investor demand: Environmental, Social, Governance (ESG) reporting is increasingly expected by institutional investors
- Cost reduction: energy efficiency, waste reduction, and resource conservation often reduce operating costs
- Talent attraction: many workers, especially younger workers, prefer employers with genuine sustainability commitments

### Ethical reasons
Businesses operate within ecosystems (physical and social). Taking more than is sustainable is a form of harm to future generations and to communities affected by environmental degradation.

## Elements of Competency

### Element 1: Identify current resource use

You need to:
- Identify what resources your work area uses (energy, water, materials, paper, equipment)
- Identify where waste is generated and how much
- Identify current sustainability policies and procedures in your organisation
- Measure or estimate your work area's resource use where possible

---

### Element 2: Identify opportunities to improve sustainability

You need to:
- Identify at least three opportunities to reduce resource use or waste in your work area
- Understand your scope — what changes can you make at your level, and what requires management action
- Consider efficiency, waste reduction, and renewable/recycled alternatives

---

### Element 3: Implement sustainable work practices

You need to:
- Apply your organisation's sustainability policies and procedures in your daily work
- Take practical steps to reduce resource use: turning off equipment, using recycled materials, reducing printing, participating in waste separation
- Support colleagues to participate in sustainability practices
- Not implement changes that are outside your authority without approval

---

### Element 4: Monitor and report on sustainability practices

You need to:
- Track resource use and compare to targets or baseline
- Report on sustainability performance through your organisation's systems
- Identify where practices have improved and where further improvement is possible

## Practical Sustainability in a Business Office

| Resource | Common Waste | Better Practice |
|---|---|---|
| **Paper** | Unnecessary printing, single-sided printing, discarding documents prematurely | Print only what is needed; double-sided; digital documents and signatures; recycling bins for paper |
| **Energy** | Lights and screens left on; inefficient equipment | Switch off when not in use; power management settings on computers; LED lighting; natural light where possible |
| **Water** | Inefficient fixtures; unnecessary use | Report leaks; use efficient fixtures; avoid unnecessary water use |
| **Equipment** | Replacing functional equipment unnecessarily; not maintaining equipment | Maintain equipment to extend life; repair before replacing; responsible disposal of e-waste |
| **Consumables** | Single-use items (cups, cutlery, packaging) | Reusable alternatives; reduce purchasing of single-use items; buy recycled content products |
| **Travel** | Unnecessary vehicle trips; solo travel | Video conferencing; public transport; car pooling; consolidating errands |

## Waste Management — The Waste Hierarchy

Like the controls hierarchy in WHS, the waste hierarchy ranks approaches from most to least preferred:

1. **Avoid** — don't generate the waste in the first place (the most sustainable option)
2. **Reduce** — generate less waste by being more efficient
3. **Reuse** — use items multiple times before disposal
4. **Recycle** — process waste into new materials
5. **Recover** — extract energy from waste (e.g., waste-to-energy)
6. **Dispose** — landfill (the least preferred option, to be used only when all other options are exhausted)

## Your Role vs Management's Role

A common assessment mistake is proposing sustainability improvements that are outside the scope of a Certificate III business worker — for example, "I would install solar panels" or "I would replace all the company vehicles with electric vehicles." These are management and capital investment decisions.

Your role at this level includes:
- Complying with existing policies
- Identifying opportunities within your work area
- Reporting concerns and suggestions through the correct channels
- Encouraging colleagues to participate
- Contributing to sustainability data collection

Management's role includes:
- Setting sustainability targets
- Capital investment decisions (renewable energy, fleet changes, building upgrades)
- Policy changes
- Reporting to regulators and investors

Know the difference and demonstrate that your response is within scope.

## Worked Example

**Prompt:**

*You work as an administrative assistant at a medium-sized financial planning firm. You have noticed that the office printer runs all day even overnight, staff regularly print emails and documents unnecessarily, there are no recycling bins (only general waste bins), and the kitchen has no recycling for bottles and cans. Your manager has asked you to identify sustainability improvements in your work area and propose practical steps you can take at your level.*

---

**Exemplary Response:**

**Resource use issues identified:**
1. **Energy waste:** The printer runs continuously, including overnight, when it is not in use. Most modern printers have a power management/sleep mode function that can be enabled.
2. **Paper waste:** Unnecessary printing of emails and documents increases both paper consumption and printer energy use.
3. **Waste management gap:** No paper recycling bins means recyclable paper is going to landfill. No recycling for bottles and cans in the kitchen has the same outcome.

**Practical steps within my scope and authority:**

*Printer power management:* I would check the printer settings and enable the sleep/power-off after inactivity function, set it to turn off at the end of the working day and on at the start. I would confirm with my manager before making changes to shared IT settings. Cost: nil.

*Reduce unnecessary printing:* I would draft a brief guide on setting default print settings to double-sided and suggest to my manager that a "print-to-file" default be set for PDFs. I would also suggest a team norm: "email is not printed unless needed for a specific meeting or regulatory purpose." This requires a conversation — not just putting up a poster.

*Recycling bins:* I would request that recycling bins be ordered for paper waste (one per desk or shared area, alongside general waste) and for kitchen recyclables. I would ask my manager to include this in the next supply order and identify the recycling collection service available to us. Cost: minimal.

**What I would NOT do without management approval:**
- Suggest changing the office to renewable energy (capital/procurement decision)
- Change the procurement policy for stationery suppliers (management decision)
- Publicly commit the organisation to sustainability targets (requires management/director authority)

**Reporting:**
I would document the changes made and, after one month, check whether paper use and printer energy consumption have changed. I would report back to my manager with observations.

**Examiner Annotation:** This response demonstrates understanding of triple-bottom-line sustainability in a practical context, applies the waste hierarchy implicitly (avoid/reduce printing before recycling), stays clearly within scope (makes or proposes low-cost, low-authority changes), includes a monitoring/reporting step, and correctly identifies what requires management approval. Strong Competent.
