## What This Qualification Is

The Certificate III in Screen and Media provides foundational skills for people working in screen production, digital media, broadcast, and related creative technology industries. It covers content creation, media production, digital storytelling, and the technical and creative skills needed to work in a production environment.

## The Screen and Media Industry

### Industry Sectors
- **Film and television production:** Feature films, television drama, documentary, news
- **Corporate and commercial production:** Training videos, marketing content, events coverage
- **Digital content:** Online video, podcasts, social media content, streaming platforms
- **Broadcasting:** Radio, television, and online broadcast
- **Post-production:** Editing, sound mixing, colour grading, visual effects
- **Animation:** 2D, 3D, motion graphics

### The Freelance Reality
The screen and media industry has a high proportion of freelance and contract workers. Many practitioners work project-to-project across multiple clients and production companies. Certificate III graduates commonly enter the industry through:
- Entry-level production roles (runner, production assistant)
- Internships and work experience with production companies
- Building independent content creation portfolios
- Working in-house for corporate communications teams

## How You Will Be Assessed

Assessment in screen and media qualifications is heavily project-based:

| Method | What It Tests |
|---|---|
| Portfolio of work | Completed media productions demonstrating technical and creative skills |
| Practical demonstration | Performing tasks (operating cameras, recording sound, editing footage) to industry standard |
| Project outcomes | Finished products (short films, edited sequences, recorded audio, photo series) assessed against brief |
| Knowledge questions | Copyright, industry practices, technical standards |
| Peer review and critique | Receiving and applying feedback; critiquing others' work constructively |

**A screen and media qualification cannot be completed through written assessment alone.** The portfolio of produced work is the primary evidence.

## Copyright in Screen and Media

Copyright is a constant issue in media production. Key concepts:

### Copyright Ownership in Production

**Who owns copyright in a production?**
- Generally, the **commissioning party** (the client, broadcaster, or studio) owns copyright in commissioned work
- The **producer** typically holds copyright in independently produced work
- Individual contributors (directors, writers, composers) may retain **moral rights** even when copyright is assigned — the right to be attributed and to object to derogatory treatment of their work

### Music in Productions

Music copyright is one of the most complex areas for media producers:
- **Synchronisation rights:** Permission to use a piece of music in a video production
- **Master recording rights:** Permission to use a specific recorded version (separate from the song itself)
- **Performance rights:** Rights to publicly perform the music (relevant for broadcast)

**Practical advice:** Unless music is specifically licensed for production use (stock music libraries, Creative Commons music, commissioned original music), do not include it in a production without clearing the rights. Music piracy in commercial productions is a serious legal risk.

### Creative Commons

Creative Commons (CC) licences allow creators to share their work with certain permissions pre-granted. Common CC licence types:
- **CC BY:** Attribution required; otherwise free use
- **CC BY-SA:** Attribution + ShareAlike (derivative works must carry same licence)
- **CC BY-NC:** Attribution + NonCommercial only
- **CC BY-ND:** Attribution + NoDerivatives (cannot modify the work)
- **CC0:** Public domain dedication — no rights reserved

Always check the specific CC licence before using material in a commercial production.

## Working in a Production Team

Screen and media productions are team endeavours. Understanding roles and the production hierarchy is essential:

### Production Roles
| Role | Responsibilities |
|---|---|
| **Executive Producer** | Overall creative and financial control |
| **Producer** | Day-to-day management of the production; logistics, budget, schedule |
| **Director** | Creative vision; directing talent and crew during production |
| **Director of Photography (DOP)** | Camera, lighting, visual style |
| **Camera Operator** | Operating camera under DOP direction |
| **Sound Recordist** | Location sound recording |
| **Editor** | Post-production assembly and finishing |
| **Production Assistant (PA)** | Administrative and logistical support |
| **Runner** | Entry-level support role; tasks as directed |

### Production Phases
1. **Development:** Script, concept, financing
2. **Pre-production:** Planning, location scouting, casting, scheduling, budgeting
3. **Production:** Principal photography/recording
4. **Post-production:** Editing, sound mixing, colour grading, VFX, delivery
5. **Distribution:** Release, broadcast, online publication

## WHS in Screen and Media Production

Media productions involve real physical risks often not associated with creative work:
- **Location safety:** Uncontrolled environments (outdoor locations, heights, traffic)
- **Electrical:** Lighting rigs and production equipment
- **Manual handling:** Camera equipment, lighting stands, cabling
- **Fatigue:** Long production days create fatigue-related risks
- **Working at height:** Rigging lights, aerial shooting
- **Talent/performer safety:** Child performers, stunt work, special effects

All productions must have a WHS plan. Students must demonstrate WHS awareness in practical assessments.
