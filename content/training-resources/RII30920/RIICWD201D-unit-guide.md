## What This Unit Is About

This is the foundation practical unit — the physical work of civil construction before specialisation.

It covers the activities that appear on almost every civil job regardless of what is being built: preparing a site, working with plant, placing and compacting material, installing pipe and drainage, handling concrete, and reinstating what you disturbed.

RIICWD201D asks you to perform these activities safely, to specification, and in the correct sequence.

## Site Preparation

### Establishing the Site

**In order:**

1. **Confirm the work area** against the drawings and the permit — boundaries, extent, restrictions
2. **Establish access and egress** for plant, deliveries and people, separated where practicable
3. **Install traffic management** per the approved TMP where the work affects a road (see RIIWHS302D)
4. **Fence and secure** the work area, particularly where the public has access
5. **Locate and mark services** — Before You Dig, physical location, marking (see RIIWHS201E). **This precedes any ground disturbance**
6. **Install erosion and sediment controls before disturbing ground**, not after
7. **Establish set-out** — survey control, offsets, levels, profiles
8. **Site facilities** — amenities, first aid, spill kit, fire extinguisher, muster point

**Two of these are routinely done in the wrong order:** sediment control installed after the ground is opened, and services marked after the excavator arrives. Both are more expensive to fix than to sequence correctly.

### Erosion and Sediment Control

Disturbed soil moves in water. Sediment reaching a waterway is an environmental offence with real penalties.

| Control | Purpose |
|---|---|
| **Silt fence** | Filters sheet flow; trapped sediment settles behind it |
| **Sediment basin** | Detains runoff so sediment settles before discharge |
| **Rock check dam** | Slows flow in a channel or drain |
| **Stabilised site access** | Rock pad preventing mud tracking onto the road |
| **Drain inlet protection** | Prevents sediment entering the stormwater system |
| **Diversion bund or drain** | Directs clean upslope water around the work area |
| **Stockpile protection** | Cover or bund; stockpiles are a major sediment source |
| **Progressive stabilisation** | Revegetate or cover as soon as an area is finished |

**Controls must be maintained.** A silt fence full of sediment stops working, and a stabilised access pad clogged with mud tracks mud. **Inspect after every rain event** and repair before the next one.

**Clean water diversion is the highest-value control** — water that never enters the disturbed area does not need treating.

## Working Around Plant

Being struck by mobile plant is a leading cause of civil construction fatalities. If you take one thing from this unit, take this section.

### The Rules

- **Never approach operating plant without positive acknowledgement.** Make eye contact with the operator and receive a clear signal back. **Never assume you have been seen**
- **Stay out of the operating radius.** An excavator's slew radius is a no-go zone, including behind the machine where the counterweight swings
- **Understand blind spots.** Large plant has substantial blind areas, particularly immediately behind and to the operator's right
- **Hi-vis does not make you visible in a blind spot.** It is necessary and it is not sufficient
- **Approach from the front, in the operator's field of view**, never from behind
- **One signaller per machine** (see RIICOM201D)
- **If you lose sight of the operator, or they lose sight of you, movement stops**
- **Never walk or stand under a raised bucket or suspended load**
- **Never ride on plant** unless it is designed and fitted for a passenger

### Spotters

A spotter is used where the operator's vision is restricted — reversing, working near services, working close to a structure or an excavation edge.

**The spotter must:**
- Be trained for the role
- Be in the operator's line of sight, or in radio contact
- Stand in a **position of safety with an escape route** — not between the machine and a fixed object
- Have authority to stop the movement, and use it
- Have no other duties while spotting

### Plant Prestart

Plant is inspected before each shift, and the check is recorded.

Typical items: fluid levels, leaks, tyres or tracks, controls, lights, alarms and cameras, mirrors, seatbelt, ROPS/FOPS integrity, hydraulic hoses, attachment security, fire extinguisher, emergency stop.

**A defect means the machine is tagged out**, not "watched".

## Excavation

Covered in detail in RIIWHS201E. The essentials for this unit:

- **Services located and marked** before any ground disturbance; **non-destructive methods within the exclusion distance** of a located service
- **Support** — batter, shore or shield according to depth, ground conditions and the SWMS. **A competent person determines the method**
- **Spoil at least 1m back from the edge** — spoil at the edge surcharges the wall and is a leading cause of collapse
- **Plant back from the edge** — machine weight and vibration destabilise the face
- **Safe access and egress** — ladder within the required distance of any person in the excavation
- **Reinspect** daily before entry, after rain, and after any change
- **Edge protection** where there is a fall risk

## Placing and Compacting Material

### Layer Thickness

Material is placed and compacted in **layers of specified maximum thickness** — commonly 150–300mm compacted, depending on the material and the compaction plant.

**Why the limit exists:** compactive effort does not penetrate indefinitely. A layer placed too thick compacts at the top and remains loose at the bottom, which tests as adequate at the surface and settles later.

**[VERIFY: maximum layer thickness is set by the project specification and the compaction plant being used. Work from the specification.]**

### Moisture

Material must be within the specified moisture range for compaction to achieve density. Too wet or too dry and the density cannot be reached regardless of roller passes. See RIICMT201D.

**Adding roller passes does not fix incorrect moisture** — a point worth restating because it is the most common misunderstanding in earthworks.

### Compaction Plant

| Plant | Suits |
|---|---|
| **Smooth drum vibrating roller** | Granular material, base course, asphalt |
| **Padfoot / sheepsfoot roller** | Cohesive soils — the feet knead the material |
| **Multi-tyred (rubber tyred) roller** | Sealing surfaces, mixed material |
| **Plate compactor** | Confined areas, trench backfill, small areas |
| **Trench roller / jumping jack (rammer)** | Trench backfill, narrow confined compaction |

**Match the plant to the material.** A smooth drum on clay will bridge and seal the surface without compacting beneath it.

### Trench Backfill

- **Bedding** placed and compacted beneath the pipe as specified
- **Haunching** — material worked in under the pipe's lower quadrants. **This is the step most often skipped and it matters** — a pipe without proper haunch support is inadequately supported and can deform or fail
- **Side fill** in layers, compacted evenly on both sides so the pipe is not displaced laterally
- **Overlay** to the specified depth above the crown **before heavy plant passes over the pipe**
- **General backfill** in layers to the surface
- **Do not compact directly over a pipe** until the specified cover is in place

## Pipe Laying

### Sequence

1. **Trench to line and level**, with the invert set to the design gradient (see CPCCCM2006 for levelling principles)
2. **Trim the trench base** to grade — a pipe laid on an uneven base bears on points rather than uniformly
3. **Place and compact bedding** to the specified depth and material
4. **Lay pipe from the downstream end, working upstream**, with sockets facing upstream. This means each spigot enters the socket in the direction of flow and the joint is not working against the flow
5. **Check line and level for every pipe**, not every third one. An error is far cheaper to correct before the next pipe is laid
6. **Make the joint** per the manufacturer's method — clean, correctly lubricated, fully home, rubber ring seated
7. **Haunch and side fill** before moving on
8. **Test** as specified — pressure test, air test, or CCTV inspection

### Common Faults

| Fault | Cause |
|---|---|
| **Ponding in the line** | Invert level error; insufficient or reversed fall |
| **Joint leaking** | Dirty joint, missing or displaced rubber ring, insufficient lubricant, joint not fully home |
| **Pipe deformation** | Inadequate haunching or side support; heavy plant over insufficient cover |
| **Pipe displaced laterally** | Uneven side fill compaction |
| **Pipe bearing on a point** | Trench base not trimmed; a rock left under the pipe |

**Excessive fall is a fault as well as insufficient fall.** In a gravity sewer, a very steep grade causes the liquid to run away from the solids, leaving them behind to build up and block the line.

## Concrete Work

### Before the Pour

- **Formwork** secure, to line and level, and adequately braced. **Formwork failure during a pour is a serious incident** — concrete is heavy and moves fast
- **Reinforcement** correctly placed, tied, and on the specified **cover** spacers. Cover is not cosmetic — it protects the steel from corrosion, and inadequate cover is the primary cause of concrete cancer
- **Base prepared** — compacted, and dampened where specified
- **Penetrations and cast-in items** positioned
- **Access** for the truck and pump, and a wash-out area
- **Confirm the delivery** — grade, slump, volume, batch time

### During the Pour

- **Place continuously** to avoid a cold joint
- **Do not add water** to the load. It increases slump and reduces strength. See RIICMT201D
- **Compact** by vibration as specified — enough to remove entrapped air, not so much that the mix segregates
- **Do not use the vibrator to move concrete** around the form; place it where it goes
- **Screed and finish** to the specified level and surface
- **Test** — slump and cylinders as required

### After the Pour

- **Cure.** This is the most neglected step. Concrete gains strength through hydration, which requires moisture and time. Concrete allowed to dry out early will not reach its design strength, and no amount of subsequent watering recovers it
- **Protect** from rain, traffic, and temperature extremes
- **Strip formwork** only after the specified time or strength
- **Wash out** into a designated containment. **Concrete washwater is highly alkaline** and must never enter a drain or waterway

## Reinstatement

Restoring the surface after underground work.

- **Backfill and compact properly** to the surface. **Reinstatement settlement is the most visible and most complained-about defect in civil work**, and it is almost always a compaction failure in the upper layers
- **Match the existing surface** — asphalt, concrete, pavers, turf — to the specification
- **Restore line and level** to match the surrounding surface. A reinstatement that ponds water or forms a trip hazard is a defect
- **Clean up** — remove all material, equipment and debris
- **Restore fences, signs, letterboxes, garden edges and anything else disturbed**
- **Photograph** before and after where practicable, particularly on private property

**Reinstatement is what the public sees and judges the whole job by.** A technically excellent drainage installation with a settled, patchy reinstatement is remembered as poor work.

## Manual Handling

Civil construction involves repetitive handling of heavy and awkward loads — pipe, plates, shoring, tools, bagged material.

- **Assess before lifting** — weight, shape, distance, grip, route, obstacles
- **Use mechanical aids** wherever possible. The excavator is a lifting device; use it
- **Two-person lifts** for heavy or awkward loads, coordinated by one person calling it
- **Technique** — close to the body, bend at the knees, no twisting, feet positioned to turn
- **Break the load down** where possible
- **Clear the route first**

**Musculoskeletal injury is the most common injury type in civil construction** and it is cumulative — the damage accrues over years and then presents as a chronic condition.

## Worked Example

**Prompt:**

*You are laying 300mm stormwater pipe in a 1.6m deep trench along a residential street. The specification requires:*

- *150mm sand bedding, compacted*
- *Haunching and side fill to 300mm above the pipe crown, in 150mm layers*
- *Compaction to 95% of standard MDD*
- *Grade 1:150*

*Your leading hand is running behind. He tells you to skip the haunching, tip the sand in around the pipe, and get the excavator to push the general backfill in so you can catch up. He says "it's only stormwater, it doesn't need to be perfect."*

*What do you do, and why does haunching matter?*

---

### Exemplary Response

**I don't skip the haunching, and I say why — because the reason is structural rather than procedural and it's the kind of thing that sounds like fussiness until you explain it.**

---

**Why haunching matters — the actual engineering**

A buried pipe does not carry the load above it on its own. **The pipe and the material around it act together as a system.**

The load from the backfill, the road surface and the traffic above is transferred into the pipe wall and then out into the surrounding material. **The haunch zone — the lower quadrants of the pipe, between the invert and the springline — is where that load transfers into the ground.**

If the haunch is not properly filled and compacted:

- **There is a void beneath the lower quadrants.** The pipe is effectively bearing on a narrow strip along its invert rather than being supported across the lower third of its circumference
- **The load concentrates.** The same load through a much smaller bearing area produces far higher stress in the pipe wall
- **The pipe deflects.** It ovalises — flattening vertically and spreading horizontally. In a flexible pipe, excessive deflection cracks the wall or fails the joints. In a rigid pipe, it cracks
- **Joints open.** Deflection and settlement pull joints apart, causing infiltration and exfiltration
- **It settles later.** Uncompacted material under the pipe consolidates over time, so the pipe drops unevenly and the grade is lost — creating ponding in a line that was laid to correct fall

**Haunching is not a finishing detail. It is the pipe's foundation.**

**"Tipping the sand in around the pipe" does not haunch it.** Sand tipped into a trench bridges across the lower quadrants and leaves a void underneath. The haunch has to be **worked in** under the pipe and compacted, deliberately, which is why it is a separate specified step.

---

**Why "it's only stormwater" is wrong**

Stormwater carries less consequence than a pressure main if it fails, which is presumably the reasoning. But:

- **It's under a residential street.** The failure mode is settlement of the road surface above the trench, which is a public safety issue and a reinstatement liability
- **A deformed or displaced joint lets groundwater in and stormwater out.** Exfiltration into the surrounding material saturates and washes out the bedding, which accelerates the settlement — it is self-worsening
- **Ponding in the line** from lost grade causes silt deposition, which builds up and blocks it. The 1:150 grade is already relatively flat; it does not have much tolerance for settlement
- **It will be tested.** CCTV inspection is standard on stormwater installation, and deflection and joint displacement show up clearly
- **Rectification means excavating the street again.** Once the road is reinstated and the surface is on, fixing a deformed pipe means re-opening the trench, at a cost far exceeding the time being saved today

---

**The other instruction — pushing backfill in with the excavator**

This is a second, separate problem.

- **It bypasses layer compaction entirely.** The specification requires 150mm layers to 95% standard MDD. Material pushed in as a mass cannot be compacted through its depth, and it will not achieve density
- **It risks displacing the pipe** laterally and vertically before it is supported
- **Heavy plant over the pipe before the specified 300mm cover is in place** can damage the pipe directly. That cover depth exists precisely to protect the pipe from construction loading
- **Reinstatement settlement** is the visible consequence, and it is the single most complained-about defect in civil work — a sunken trench line down a residential street generates complaints for years

---

**What I say**

I would not refuse flatly. He is under schedule pressure and needs a solution, not an objection.

*"I can't skip the haunch — if the lower quadrants aren't supported the pipe ovalises and the joints pull, and it'll show up on the CCTV. And it'll settle, so we'd lose the grade on a 1:150 line that hasn't got much to give. Same with pushing the backfill in — we won't make 95% and if we track over it before we've got 300 cover we could damage the pipe.*

*If we're chasing time, can we get another plate compactor or a trench roller on it, or put another person on the backfill so we're compacting as we lay rather than at the end? That'd claw back more than skipping the haunch would."*

**That gives him a way to actually recover time** rather than just telling him no.

---

**If I'm directed to proceed anyway**

I decline to do it that way, and I escalate.

**And critically — I document it.** If this pipe is built to that method and it fails a CCTV inspection or settles in two years, there will be an investigation into how it was installed. I want a record showing the specified method, the direction I received, and my response.

I would record: date, time, the instruction, the specification requirements it departed from, my response, and who I escalated to.

**This is also a non-conformance.** If any of it has already been built that way, it should be raised as a non-conformance and assessed rather than covered up — because the cheapest possible time to fix it is before the next section goes in.

---

**The judgement**

The pressure is real and it is not unreasonable — the job is behind and he is trying to recover it.

But **the time saved is measured in hours and the consequence is measured in years.** A trench line settling down a residential street, or a CCTV report showing deflected pipe and open joints, costs vastly more than the afternoon this would save — and it costs it after the crew has moved on, which is precisely why the shortcut is tempting.

**Examiner Annotation:** Outstanding. The explanation of haunching is the strongest element — the student describes the pipe and surrounding material as a load-transfer system, identifies the haunch zone as where load enters the ground, and traces the consequences of a void there through concentrated stress, ovalisation, joint displacement and eventual settlement. That is genuine structural understanding rather than procedural recall. The observation that tipped sand bridges and leaves a void, and that haunch material must be actively worked in, addresses the specific instruction accurately. The rebuttal of "it's only stormwater" is well constructed: the student identifies that exfiltration saturates and washes out the bedding, making the failure self-worsening, and correctly notes that a 1:150 grade has little tolerance for settlement. Treating the excavator backfill instruction as a separate problem, and identifying that cover depth exists to protect the pipe from construction loading, shows the student is reading the specification as a set of connected requirements. Offering a realistic time-recovery alternative is professionally intelligent. The documentation and non-conformance response is correct and includes the important point that rectification is cheapest before the next section is built. Strong Competent.
