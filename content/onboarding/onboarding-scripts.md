## 4. FIRST INTELLIGENCE REPORT WALKTHROUGH SCRIPT

*Delivered by AIIPD Intelligence Partner in a 30-minute video or live session.*

---

"Welcome to the walkthrough of your first AIIPD Intelligence Brief.

The brief in front of you is a Council Intelligence Profile for [Organisation Name]. I want to walk you through how to read it, what each section is showing you, and how to use it most effectively in your leadership work.

**Cover page and intelligence date**

You'll notice the intelligence date in the top right corner — this tells you when this brief was generated and which version of the integrated intelligence it draws on. Intelligence briefs are point-in-time syntheses — the underlying intelligence updates continuously, but the brief reflects the state of the intelligence at the date of generation.

**Executive Intelligence Summary**

The first two pages are the Executive Intelligence Summary — designed for CEO and elected member audiences who need the essential intelligence quickly. I recommend you read this first, and then decide which sections of the full brief are most relevant to your current priorities.

The summary highlights [key finding 1], [key finding 2], and [key finding 3] — three intelligence findings that our analysis identified as most significant for [Organisation Name]'s current context. These are grounded in the evidence detailed in the body of the brief.

**Intelligence dimensions**

Each subsequent section covers one intelligence dimension. Let's look at the Governance Intelligence section as an example. [Navigate to section.] You'll see that the analysis draws on [evidence sources] — this is listed in the evidence panel on the right side. Below the narrative analysis, there are supporting data visualisations — in this case, a decision pattern analysis and a governance framework assessment.

You'll notice there are no scores or rankings in this section. AIIPD doesn't reduce governance quality to a number — because a number can't tell you what the patterns in council decision-making reveal about the organisation's governance culture. The narrative analysis can.

**Comparative intelligence context**

Towards the end of the brief, you'll find the Comparative Place Intelligence section. This is where [Organisation Name]'s intelligence picture is contextualised against comparable organisations. This helps you understand what is specific to your situation and what is common across similar organisations — which has very different implications for how you respond.

**How to use this brief**

I recommend three specific uses for this brief:

First, as a pre-reading for your next leadership team strategy session. The brief gives your team a shared, evidence-grounded picture of where the organisation is — which is the foundation for a productive strategic conversation.

Second, as the basis for an elected member briefing. The Executive Summary is designed to be shared with the Mayor and councillors. It gives them the intelligence they need without requiring them to read the full document.

Third, as the evidence foundation for your next strategic planning cycle. The intelligence signals at the end of the brief are specifically designed to inform what your planning needs to address.

We'll generate an updated brief each quarter, and your Intelligence Partner will alert you when new intelligence has been integrated. Any questions about this brief can be directed to [Partner Name] directly.

Thank you for your time — I hope this is the start of an intelligence relationship that genuinely supports [Organisation Name]'s leadership work."

## 6. GETTING STARTED GUIDE

*Structured step-by-step guide for new users.*

---

### AIIPD Civic Intelligence™ — Getting Started Guide

**Welcome to AIIPD Civic Intelligence™**

This guide will help you move from first login to your first intelligence outputs — in four structured steps.

---

**Step 1: Complete Your Intelligence Profile**
*Time required: 10 minutes*

When you first log in, you will be prompted to complete your Intelligence Profile. This configures your experience for your role and intelligence priorities.

- Select your role (CEO / Director / Strategic Planner / Elected Member / Consultant / Researcher / Other)
- Confirm your primary organisation
- Select your top three intelligence priorities (from: Governance Intelligence / Financial Intelligence / Community Intelligence / Infrastructure Intelligence / Strategic Foresight / Comparative Intelligence / Emerging Risks)
- Set your notification preferences

Your Intelligence Profile ensures that your Civic Intelligence Centre surfaces the most relevant intelligence for your work.

---

**Step 2: Review Your Intelligence Summary**
*Time required: 15–30 minutes*

Navigate to the Intelligence Summary in your Civic Intelligence Centre. This is your current intelligence picture — synthesised across all dimensions for your primary organisation.

Read through each intelligence dimension panel. Click "View full intelligence" on any dimension to see the detailed analysis and evidence behind the summary.

Note any intelligence signals flagged in the signals panel — these are conditions AIIPD has identified as emerging or requiring attention.

---

**Step 3: Open Your Council Intelligence Profile**
*Time required: 30–45 minutes*

Navigate to Intelligence Briefs and open your Council Intelligence Profile. This is your comprehensive intelligence portrait.

Read the Executive Intelligence Summary first (pages 1–2). This gives you the key intelligence findings.

Then review the intelligence dimensions most relevant to your current priorities. Each section includes narrative analysis, supporting data visualisations, and evidence references.

If you want to understand the methodology behind any analysis, click the methodology note in the relevant section.

---

**Step 4: Explore Comparative Intelligence**
*Time required: 20–30 minutes*

Navigate to Comparative Place Intelligence and explore your peer group. Your comparator group has been pre-configured based on your organisation's contextual characteristics — but you can adjust it at any time.

Select two or three comparator organisations and explore the comparative intelligence view. Look for:
- Dimensions where your organisation is tracking differently from comparators
- Approaches that comparators are taking that might be relevant to your context
- Common challenges across your peer group that suggest sector-wide rather than organisation-specific responses

---

**You are ready.**

By completing these four steps, you have established the intelligence foundation for your ongoing use of AIIPD. Your Intelligence Partner will check in with you in week two to discuss what you found and how AIIPD can best support your specific intelligence needs.
