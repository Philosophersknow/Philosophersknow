## The Commercial Idea

An RTO subscribes. They receive resource packs that carry **their** logo, name and RTO number alongside the AIIPD mark, so the material presents to their students as part of their own delivery — while copyright, the content and the underlying IP remain yours.

**This is a strong model.** RTOs want material that looks like theirs; building it themselves is expensive and they know it. Co-branding removes the main psychological objection to buying external resources — that students will see they came from somewhere else.

**It also multiplies your exposure**, because your content will be distributed under someone else's brand, to students you have no relationship with, possibly after being edited. The licence is what manages that, and it needs to be right before the first pack ships.

---

# 1. What Is Actually Being Licensed

**Be precise about this in the licence, because it is where disputes start.**

| Element | Position |
|---|---|
| **Copyright in the content** | **Retained by you.** Never assigned, never shared |
| **The AIIPD name, logo and trade marks** | **Retained by you.** Licensee gets no rights in them beyond displaying them as required |
| **Right to use the material** | Licensed — non-exclusive, non-transferable, revocable, term-limited |
| **Right to apply the licensee's own branding** | Licensed, **within the defined co-brand zone only** |
| **Right to distribute to the licensee's own enrolled students** | Licensed |
| **Right to modify content** | **See Section 4 — recommend: no** |
| **Right to sub-licence, resell or distribute beyond own students** | **Expressly prohibited** |
| **Licensee's own logo and marks** | Remain the licensee's; you get a limited right to display them in the co-branded output |

**Locked. Not editable. Present on every page.**

## 2.3 Editable vs locked — the definitive list

| Element | Status |
|---|---|
| Licensee logo (cover, defined size and position) | ✅ **Editable** |
| Licensee name, RTO code, contact details, website | ✅ **Editable** |
| Licensee-supplied cover colour from an approved palette | ✅ **Editable** |
| A licensee introduction page **inserted before** the content | ✅ **Editable** — but must not alter or contradict the content |
| Unit content, worked examples, examiner annotations | 🔒 **Locked** |
| The AIIPD logo and its position | 🔒 **Locked** |
| The copyright notice | 🔒 **Locked** |
| The disclaimer page, in full | 🔒 **Locked** |
| Page footers | 🔒 **Locked** |
| Version and date fields | 🔒 **Locked** |
| "Developed by" attribution | 🔒 **Locked** |
| Licence reference | 🔒 **Locked** |

**Removing or obscuring any locked element is a breach of licence and an infringement of copyright and moral rights.** State it in those terms.

## 2.4 Moral rights

Under the *Copyright Act 1968* (Cth), the author has **moral rights** — including the right of attribution and the right against derogatory treatment. These are **personal and cannot be assigned**.

**Use this.** The licence should require attribution and should record that the licensee **consents to specified acts** (applying its branding, reproducing for its students) while acknowledging that removal of attribution is **both a licence breach and a moral rights infringement**. That gives you two causes of action rather than one.

# 3. Licence Terms — Outline for Drafting

**This is a brief for a lawyer, not a licence.**

### 3.1 Grant
Non-exclusive · non-transferable · non-sublicensable · revocable · limited to the term · limited to the specified qualifications · limited to the licensee's own currently enrolled students at its own registered scope.

### 3.2 Permitted use
- Reproduce and distribute to **its own enrolled students** for their study
- Use internally for trainer reference, assessor calibration and validation
- Apply its branding within the co-brand zone
- Retain student copies already issued after termination *(see 3.7)*

### 3.3 Prohibited use
- Sub-licensing, resale, or supply to any other organisation
- Distribution beyond its own enrolled students
- Use as, or incorporation into, a **validated assessment tool** *(see Section 5)*
- Removal, obscuring or alteration of any locked element
- Modification of content *(see Section 4)*
- Use after termination or expiry, other than as permitted in 3.7
- Claiming authorship, or representing the material as developed by the licensee
- Use in a way that suggests AIIPD endorses the licensee's training or accreditation status

### 3.4 Licensee warranties
- It is a currently registered RTO with the qualification on its scope
- It will use the material only within that scope
- It will not represent the material as accredited training or as a validated assessment tool

### 3.5 Attribution
Locked elements displayed on all copies, in all formats, including any derived format the licensee produces (LMS upload, print, slide).

### 3.6 Term and renewal
Aligned to the subscription. **Lapsed payment ends the licence.**

### 3.7 Termination — the clause that needs the most thought

**Realistically you cannot recall material already in students' hands.** Do not draft as though you can.

Suggested structure:
- On termination, the licensee **ceases all further reproduction and distribution immediately**
- Copies **already issued** to students enrolled before termination may be retained by those students for the duration of their enrolment
- The licensee **destroys or returns** master files and unissued copies
- The licensee **removes** the material from any LMS, portal or shared drive within a defined period
- Attribution and copyright obligations **survive** termination

### 3.8 Liability and indemnity

Two directions, and both matter:

**Your liability to the licensee** — mirrors the disclaimer, with the ACL carve-out. Note that an RTO is likely a **business** rather than a consumer, which may give more room to limit than in the student contract. **[VERIFY with your solicitor.]**

**Licensee indemnity to you** — the licensee indemnifies you against claims arising from: its use of the material outside the licence; any modification it makes; its representations about accreditation status; and its own training delivery.

**This is the clause that protects you when an RTO puts your content in front of students under its own logo and something goes wrong.** It is not optional.

### 3.9 Audit
Right to request evidence of compliance — student numbers, distribution method, format used.

# 4. Should Licensees Be Allowed to Edit Content?

**Recommendation: no, in the standard tier.**

**The case for allowing it:** RTOs want to contextualise for their cohort, their state, their industry links. It is a genuine and reasonable want.

**The case against, which is stronger:**

| Risk | Detail |
|---|---|
| **Liability** | An RTO edits a confined space or brake inspection guide, introduces an error, a student is harmed. Your logo is on it |
| **Quality** | The examiner annotations and worked examples are the product. Edited badly, they stop working and your brand carries it |
| **Moral rights** | Derogatory treatment of the work is a moral rights infringement, but litigating it is worse than preventing it |
| **Version control** | You cannot maintain currency across an unknown number of divergent forks |
| **Currency** | When a training package updates, you push an update. Forked copies do not receive it |

**Recommended structure:**

| Tier | Editing |
|---|---|
| **Standard** | No content editing. Co-brand zone only. Licensee may **insert** its own pages before or after, clearly delineated |
| **Contextualised** *(premium)* | You make agreed amendments **on their behalf**, for a fee. You retain control, they get contextualisation, currency is maintained |
| **Custom development** | Bespoke material, separately quoted, separately licensed |

**The premium tier is the better commercial answer anyway** — it converts a licence concession into a revenue line.

# 5. The Assessment Tool Boundary — State It Loudly

**This is the single largest compliance risk in the RTO market and it must be unmissable in the licence.**

An RTO's assessment tools must be **validated for its own context, cohort and training and assessment strategy**. Nothing bought off the shelf satisfies that.

**If an RTO takes a co-branded worked example, puts it in an assessment tool, and an auditor finds it, the finding lands on them — but the conversation lands on you**, and your reputation in a small market is the asset.

**Therefore:**

- Licence prohibits use as, or incorporation into, an assessment tool
- The disclaimer says so
- The cover of every co-branded pack says so
- The RTO warrants it understands the distinction

**Say it in the sales conversation too.** Being the supplier who is straight about this is a differentiator — the market has been sold "compliant assessment tools" by people who could not deliver them, and RTOs have been burned.

# 6. Technical Implementation

## 6.1 Architecture

**Source content stays single-source. Branding is applied at build time.**

```
content/training-resources/           ← single source of truth, unbranded
        ↓
  build pipeline
        ↓
  + brand profile (JSON per licensee)
  + disclaimer block (locked)
  + attribution block (locked)
        ↓
  branded PDF output per licensee
```

**Never maintain a branded copy of the content per licensee.** That is how forks and currency failures happen. One source, many outputs.

## 6.2 Brand profile

```json
{
  "licence_ref": "AIIPD-CB-2026-0014",
  "licensee_name": "Example Training Group Pty Ltd",
  "rto_code": "12345",
  "logo_path": "brands/example/logo.png",
  "contact": "training@example.edu.au",
  "website": "example.edu.au",
  "accent_colour": "#1F4E5F",
  "qualifications_licensed": ["CHC33021", "BSB30120"],
  "term_start": "2026-08-01",
  "term_end": "2027-07-31",
  "tier": "standard"
}
```

**Logo requirements to specify:** minimum resolution, transparent PNG or SVG, defined maximum dimensions, safe area. Ask for a vector file.

## 6.3 Build-time enforcement

The pipeline should make breach difficult by construction:

- Disclaimer page **injected**, not optional
- Footer **injected** on every page
- Attribution block **injected** on the cover
- **Watermark** every page with the licence reference — visible or subtle. This makes leaked material traceable to a licensee
- **Restrict PDF permissions** — no editing, no content extraction. Not a strong control, but it raises the effort and evidences intent
- Build **fails** if `term_end` has passed
- Build **fails** if the qualification is not in `qualifications_licensed`

**The watermark is the most valuable of these.** If a co-branded pack turns up on a file sharing site, you know whose it was.

## 6.4 Output formats

| Format | Notes |
|---|---|
| **PDF** | Primary. Watermarked, permission-restricted, disclaimer embedded |
| **Print-ready PDF** | Higher resolution, bleed, CMYK if commercially printed |
| **LMS package (SCORM)** | Later. Note it makes extraction easier — price accordingly |
| **Editable source** | **Do not supply.** This is the whole point of the model |

# 7. Pricing

Co-branding is a **premium** on the base RTO subscription — it is worth more to them than plain material and it costs you setup effort.

| Tier | What they get | Indicative |
|---|---|---|
| **Standard RTO subscription** | Unbranded material | Per `RTO-RESOURCES-MARKETING-PLAN.md` §9 |
| **Co-branded — Standard** | Their branding, locked content, up to N qualifications | Base **+ 40–60%** |
| **Co-branded — Contextualised** | Above, plus agreed amendments made by you | Base **+ 100–150%** |
| **Co-branded — Enterprise** | All qualifications, priority updates, multi-campus | Negotiated |
| **One-off setup fee** | Brand profile creation, logo integration, first build | **$500–$1,500** |

**The setup fee matters.** It covers real work and it filters out RTOs who want co-branding as a free extra rather than because it has value to them.

# 8. Sales Positioning

**What to lead with:**

> *"Your logo, your RTO code, your students see your material. Written by someone who knows what an assessor is actually looking for, maintained as training packages change, and you never have to build or update it."*

**The three objections and the answers:**

| Objection | Answer |
|---|---|
| *"Our competitors will have the same material"* | True, and worth saying plainly. **Non-exclusive is what makes it affordable.** Exclusivity by qualification and region can be a premium tier if genuinely wanted, priced accordingly |
| *"Can we use it as our assessment tools?"* | **No — and here is why that protects you.** See Section 5. Being straight here builds more trust than a vague yes |
| *"What if the training package changes?"* | **We update the source and you receive the updated build.** That is the strongest argument in the whole pitch — it is the thing in-house development fails at |

# 9. What To Do Before the First Co-Branded Pack Ships

1. **Solicitor drafts the licence** from Section 3. Do not ship on a handshake
2. **Confirm trade mark position** for AIIPD — the model asserts marks. **[VERIFY: IP Australia registration status]**
3. **Resolve image licensing** — every image must be originated or fully assigned. See the disclaimer suite, Section 6
4. **Build the pipeline** with locked elements injected, not optional
5. **Complete the training package verification** — a co-branded pack under an RTO's logo with a wrong qualification code is materially worse than the same error unbranded. Note that two such errors were found on 30 July 2026
6. **Decide the editing policy** and price the tiers
7. **Draft the RTO onboarding pack** — brand asset requirements, what they can and cannot change, and why
