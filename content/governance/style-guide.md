# AIIPD Civic Intelligence™ — Editorial Style Guide

**Purpose:** Complete editorial standards for all AIIPD content — website, platform, reports, communications, proposals, and marketing. This guide is authoritative. Apply it consistently across all content creation, editing, and review.

**Version:** 1.0 | June 2026

---

## PART 1: BRAND VOICE AND TONE

### Core Voice Attributes

**Authoritative without being arrogant.** AIIPD speaks from a position of deep expertise and genuine analytical capability. It does not shout or oversell. It states with confidence what the evidence supports and acknowledges with honesty what the evidence does not resolve.

**Clear without being simple.** The audience is intelligent, educated, and professional. AIIPD does not condescend or over-explain. It communicates complex ideas clearly — without unnecessary jargon, but without dumbing down content that deserves analytical depth.

**Evidence-grounded without being cold.** AIIPD is about people, communities, and the decisions that shape civic life. Rigour and humanity are not in tension — they are both essential. Intelligence that does not ultimately serve people is not worth generating.

**Forward-looking without being speculative.** AIIPD distinguishes between what the evidence supports (stated with confidence), what the evidence suggests (stated with appropriate qualification), and what remains uncertain (acknowledged honestly). Foresight is not prediction; analysis is not certainty.

**Practical without being superficial.** Intelligence has to be usable. Every content piece should have a clear, practical application for the intended audience. Abstraction without application is not AIIPD's register.

---

### Tone by Audience Context

**Local Government Executive Leadership (CEO, Director)**
Tone: Peer-level, direct, respectful of time. Write as a trusted advisor would brief a senior executive — concise, analytical, action-oriented. No hand-holding, no padding. Trust the reader's intelligence and experience.

**Elected Officials (Mayor, Councillors)**
Tone: Accessible, civic-minded, community-oriented. Clear and jargon-free. Respectful of the democratic context. Evidence-grounded but not technically dense. Always link intelligence to community outcomes.

**State Government and Policy**
Tone: Policy-professional, evidence-rigorous, appropriately formal. Academic precision without academic inaccessibility. Reference relevant policy frameworks and governance contexts. Acknowledge the complexity of cross-jurisdictional and sector-wide policy questions.

**Consultants and Advisors**
Tone: Professional peer, analytically engaged. Intellectually credible. Acknowledge the consulting context — speed, evidence quality, client value. Not sales-y; collegial.

**Academic Researchers**
Tone: Scholarly rigour, methodological transparency, appropriate caveats. Cite evidence sources. Distinguish between established findings and emerging signals. Consistent with academic publishing conventions where relevant.

**Community Organisations**
Tone: Warm, respectful, practically oriented. Accessible language. Community dignity. Evidence that serves advocacy and community development, not evidence that judges community conditions.

## PART 2: PREFERRED TERMS AND PHRASES (A–Z GLOSSARY)

**Annual Intelligence Review** — AIIPD's annual comprehensive intelligence product. Capitalised as a proper product name.

**Civic Intelligence** — Synthesised, evidence-based intelligence about civic conditions, organisations, and places. Always capitalised when used as part of the brand name (AIIPD Civic Intelligence™). Use lowercase in general usage ("civic intelligence capability").

**Civic Intelligence Centre** — The primary user interface and intelligence workspace in the AIIPD platform. Always capitalised. Do not abbreviate to "CIC" in formal content; acceptable in UI labels only.

**Community Intelligence** — Intelligence about the people, social conditions, and community dynamics of a place. Capitalised when referring to the specific AIIPD capability (Community Intelligence Profile); lowercase in general usage.

**Comparative Place Intelligence** — AIIPD's capability for cross-jurisdictional intelligence. Always capitalised when used as a proper capability name.

**Decision support** — Preferred over "decision-making tool." AIIPD supports decisions; it does not make them.

**DRMC** — Dynamic Relational Model of Consciousness. Always spell out in full at first reference in any document. Acceptable to use abbreviation thereafter. Do not explain DRMC in detail unless the context warrants it; reference it as "AIIPD's proprietary analytical framework."

**Emerging signal** — A developing condition identified through AIIPD's intelligence monitoring. Preferred over "alert," "warning," or "risk flag."

**Evidence** — Preferred over "data." Evidence implies interpretation; data implies raw material. Use "evidence" when referring to interpreted, structured intelligence inputs.

**Evidence synthesis** — The process of drawing together evidence from multiple sources into analytical intelligence. Preferred over "data analysis" or "report generation."

**Evidence-based** — Hyphenated as a compound modifier (evidence-based decision-making). Not hyphenated as a predicate (the approach is evidence based).

**Governance Intelligence** — Intelligence about governance structures, patterns, and quality. Capitalised when referring to the AIIPD capability or product.

**Intelligence** — AIIPD's primary output concept. Use to describe what the platform generates, what users access, and what decisions are informed by. Do not overuse to the point of losing meaning — vary with synonyms (insight, understanding, evidence, analysis) where the rhythm requires.

**Intelligence Brief** — AIIPD's structured intelligence output documents. Always capitalised as a product category. Specific brief types also capitalised (Council Intelligence Profile, Financial Intelligence Review, etc.).

**Intelligence dimension** — A domain of civic intelligence analysis (governance, financial, community, infrastructure, environmental, strategic). Preferred over "metric," "indicator," or "KPI."

**Intelligence Partner** — AIIPD's term for a dedicated platform support contact. Capitalised as a role title.

**Intelligence signal** — A platform notification of an emerging condition. Preferred over "alert," "flag," or "notification."

**Longitudinal** — Preferred over "historical" when describing data spanning time. "Historical" implies pastness; "longitudinal" implies analytical tracking across time.

**Place** — A core AIIPD concept referring to the geographic, social, and civic reality of a local government area or community. Use as a concept, not just a location descriptor.

**Place Intelligence** — AIIPD's intelligence about specific places. Capitalised when referring to the platform capability or database. "Place Intelligence Database" always capitalised.

**Platform** — Use to describe AIIPD as a whole. "The AIIPD platform" or "the AIIPD Civic Intelligence platform." Preferred over "tool," "software," "system," or "product" in external communications.

**Strategic foresight** — Forward-looking intelligence about emerging trends and probable futures. Lowercase in general usage; capitalised as a product name (Strategic Foresight Report, Strategic Foresight Engine).

**Synthesis** — The analytical process of drawing evidence together into coherent intelligence. Use consistently; preferred over "analysis" when the emphasis is on integration of multiple sources.

## PART 3: TERMS TO AVOID AND THEIR REPLACEMENTS

| Avoid | Use Instead | Reason |
|-------|------------|--------|
| Benchmarking | Comparative intelligence, comparative place intelligence, evidence-based comparison | "Benchmarking" implies ranking and competition, not learning and understanding |
| Performance measurement | Intelligence, organisational intelligence | "Performance measurement" is evaluative and compliance-oriented |
| Scorecard | Intelligence profile, intelligence summary | "Scorecard" implies points-based assessment |
| Ratings | Intelligence dimensions, contextual assessment | "Ratings" implies judgment and external evaluation |
| Rankings | Contextual position, comparative intelligence | "Rankings" creates defensiveness and misrepresents intelligence value |
| Dashboard | Civic Intelligence Centre | "Dashboard" implies passive data display |
| KPIs / metrics | Intelligence dimensions | Too technical and measurement-oriented |
| Data | Evidence (in interpretive contexts) | "Data" implies raw material; "evidence" implies interpretation |
| Reports | Intelligence Briefs | "Reports" implies static documents |
| Compliance | Governance quality, accountability | "Compliance" creates punitive associations |
| Above/below average | Strong/developing in context, [more/less developed] in this dimension | Avoids stigma and ranking implications |
| High/low performer | Strong/developing in [dimension], contextually positioned | Avoids judgment framing |
| Falling short | Opportunity to strengthen, developing | Improvement orientation |
| Failing | Experiencing challenges, facing a developing condition | Dignity in language |
| Alerts | Intelligence signals | Removes alarm/crisis connotation |
| Monitor | Track, maintain intelligence attention | Less surveillance-adjacent |
| Surveillance | Intelligence monitoring (only where directly relevant) | Avoid surveillance framing entirely |
| Tool | Platform, capability | "Tool" undersells the platform |
| Software | Platform | "Software" commoditises the offering |
| Compare | Examine in context, analyse comparators | "Compare" implies competition |
| Prediction | Foresight, projection, trajectory analysis | "Prediction" overstates certainty |
| Artificial intelligence | Evidence synthesis, intelligence engine, DRMC-powered (where relevant) | Avoid AI hype language |
| Machine learning | Analytical framework, pattern recognition | Avoid technical AI jargon |
| Big data | Comprehensive evidence, Place Intelligence Database | Avoid data industry buzzwords |

## PART 5: NUMBERS AND DATA PRESENTATION

**Numbers in body text:**
- Spell out one through nine; use numerals for 10 and above
- Spell out any number that begins a sentence
- Use numerals for all data points, statistics, and measurement values regardless of size

**Percentages:** Use the % symbol with a space before it in body text (75 %). Use no space in charts and tables (75%).

**Dollar values:** Use $ symbol without space (e.g., $2.4 million, $450,000). Abbreviate millions as "m" only in charts; spell out in body text.

**Population figures:** Use numerals with commas for thousands (e.g., 48,500 residents). Avoid approximate figures without clear indication of the basis (e.g., "approximately 48,000" or "48,500 at the 2021 census").

**Dates:** Day Month Year format (3 June 2026). Do not use ordinal suffixes (not "3rd June"). Intelligence dates in briefs: "Intelligence current as at [date]."

**Data confidence indicators:** When presenting projected or estimated data, always indicate the confidence level and source basis. Use consistent language:
- "Evidence strongly suggests..." — high-confidence finding
- "Intelligence indicates..." — moderate-confidence finding
- "An emerging signal suggests..." — early-stage, lower-confidence finding
- "It is worth monitoring whether..." — speculative, insufficient evidence for a finding

**Data citations in intelligence briefs:** Source attributions should be parenthetical in the narrative and compiled in an evidence sources section at the end of every brief.

## PART 6: REPORT NAMING CONVENTIONS

**Standard format:** [Intelligence Domain] Intelligence [Product Type] — [Organisation Name] — [Intelligence Date]

**Examples:**
- Council Intelligence Profile — Wagga Wagga City Council — June 2026
- Financial Intelligence Review — City of Melbourne — Annual 2025–26
- Emerging Risk Intelligence Brief — Q2 2026 — Ballarat City Council
- Regional Intelligence Synthesis — Murray-Darling Region — 2026

**File naming convention:**
[Organisation Abbreviation]_[Brief Type Abbreviation]_[YYYY-MM]

**Examples:**
- WWCC_CIP_2026-06 (Wagga Wagga City Council — Council Intelligence Profile — June 2026)
- CoM_FIR_2026-06 (City of Melbourne — Financial Intelligence Review — June 2026)

**Internal document versioning:**
v1.0 = first published version; v1.1 = minor revision; v2.0 = major revision

## PART 7: AUDIENCE-SPECIFIC TONE ADJUSTMENTS

**For CEO audiences:** Lead with strategic implications, not data. Every paragraph should earn its place by contributing to decision-relevant understanding. Ruthless brevity. Professional but not cold.

**For Elected Members:** Plain language is not a simplification — it is a precision requirement. Elected members may have deep community knowledge but varying analytical backgrounds. Explain what intelligence means in human terms. Connect to community outcomes. Avoid internal organisational jargon.

**For Finance Directors and CFOs:** Technical precision is valued. Use correct financial terminology. Interpret technical data, but do not oversimplify for an audience that can read a balance sheet. Show your analytical work where it adds credibility.

**For Infrastructure and Engineering professionals:** Technical specificity is respected. Asset condition terminology, capital works language, and infrastructure management frameworks should be used accurately. Executive summary for the CEO; technical detail in the body for the engineers.

**For Policy makers:** Acknowledge policy complexity. Do not present intelligence as if policy questions have single correct answers. Present evidence and implications; support informed judgment rather than prescribing action.

**For researchers:** Methodological transparency is paramount. Document analytical approaches, data sources, quality limitations, and confidence levels. Academic citation conventions where publishing.

## PART 8: ON-BRAND VS. OFF-BRAND LANGUAGE EXAMPLES

*Ten pairs showing the transformation from off-brand to on-brand language.*

---

**Pair 1**
Off-brand: "Your council scored 67 out of 100 on governance compliance."
On-brand: "Governance intelligence for [Organisation Name] indicates a well-structured framework with notable strengths in decision transparency, alongside an opportunity to develop consistency in accountability reporting."

---

**Pair 2**
Off-brand: "Your financial performance is below average for comparable councils."
On-brand: "Financial intelligence signals that [Organisation Name]'s current revenue structure creates a contextual vulnerability in a declining rate base environment — a pattern shared by several comparable organisations, suggesting a structural challenge rather than an organisational deficit."

---

**Pair 3**
Off-brand: "Community satisfaction scores have declined three years running."
On-brand: "Community intelligence indicates a developing gap between community expectation of service responsiveness and current service delivery capacity — a signal that warrants leadership attention and deeper investigation through the Community Intelligence Profile."

---

**Pair 4**
Off-brand: "Click here to start your free trial."
On-brand: "Request a Platform Briefing — a 30-minute structured conversation with the AIIPD Intelligence Team about your organisation's intelligence priorities."

---

**Pair 5**
Off-brand: "AIIPD's algorithm calculates a performance score for each council using AI."
On-brand: "AIIPD's Evidence Synthesis Engine applies the DRMC analytical framework to synthesise evidence from multiple civic intelligence sources into structured intelligence outputs."

---

**Pair 6**
Off-brand: "See where your council ranks among its peers."
On-brand: "Understand how your organisation relates to comparable jurisdictions — surfacing patterns, shared challenges, and approaches worth understanding."

---

**Pair 7**
Off-brand: "Download your benchmarking report."
On-brand: "Generate your Intelligence Brief — a structured, evidence-based intelligence product tailored to your audience and purpose."

---

**Pair 8**
Off-brand: "AIIPD monitors council performance and alerts you to problems."
On-brand: "AIIPD's intelligence monitoring continuously integrates new source data, identifies emerging conditions, and surfaces intelligence signals that warrant leadership attention."

---

**Pair 9**
Off-brand: "Top-performing councils in this area are doing X."
On-brand: "Comparable organisations that have developed strong capability in this dimension have generally approached it through X — an approach that may be worth examining in [Organisation Name]'s context."

---

**Pair 10**
Off-brand: "Your infrastructure is underperforming relative to peers."
On-brand: "Infrastructure intelligence for [Organisation Name] indicates a developing asset renewal gap, consistent with patterns seen in comparable organisations that deferred capital investment during [period]. The intelligence suggests [strategic implication]."
