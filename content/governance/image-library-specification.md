## The Governing Constraint

**Every image in this library must be originally created or fully assigned.**

This is not a preference. It follows directly from the co-branding model:

1. You license material to an RTO
2. The RTO applies its branding
3. The RTO distributes it to its students

**Standard stock image licences prohibit sub-licensing and redistribution** — which is precisely what steps 2 and 3 are. A stock photograph in a co-branded pack is a breach per image, per licensee, per distribution.

See `disclaimer-and-copyright-suite.md` Section 6.

**Therefore: generate diagrams from code, commission with full assignment, or do without.**

---

## Why Code-Generated Diagrams Are the Right Answer Here

Beyond the licensing position, they are simply better for this material:

| | Code-generated diagram | Stock photograph |
|---|---|---|
| **Ownership** | Outright | Licensed, restricted |
| **Teaching value** | Shows the concept — a clearance calculation, a control hierarchy | Shows a person in a hard hat |
| **Consistency** | Identical house style across 90 documents | Varies by source |
| **Correction** | Edit a number, regenerate | Re-source, re-license |
| **Currency** | Regenerate when a standard changes | Obsolete image stays obsolete |
| **Cost** | Zero marginal | Per image, per licence tier |

**A photograph of a trench teaches nothing. A cross-section showing spoil setback, plant setback, shield and ladder access teaches the unit.**

Photographs have a place — a real fault, a real wear pattern, a real defect — but that is **commissioned photography with assignment**, not stock.

## House Style

Defined in the script and applied automatically.

### Palette

| Token | Hex | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| `DANGER` | `#B3261E` | Stop · fatal · prohibited |
| `WARN` | `#B26A00` | Caution · elevated risk |
| `SAFE` | `#1F6F43` | Safe · permitted |
| `INFO` | `#1F4E5F` | Neutral technical |
| `ACCENT` | `#8C6D1F` | Highlight |
| `INK` / `MUTED` / `RULE` | `#1A1A1A` / `#6B6B6B` / `#D4D4D4` | Text and structure |

**Chosen to remain distinguishable in greyscale print and for common colour vision deficiencies.** RTOs print in mono more often than they admit, and a diagram that relies on red-versus-green to convey meaning fails twice.

**Colour never carries meaning alone** — every colour-coded element is also labelled.

### Typography and format

- **Liberation Sans** — metrically compatible with Arial, freely licensed, available on the build machine
- **300 DPI PNG** — print-capable and screen-usable
- Consistent title, subtitle, footer rule and attribution on every diagram
- **Copyright line burned into every image** — so a diagram extracted from a PDF still carries attribution

### Layout rule

**All content must sit within y = 6..86 on the internal 0–100 grid.** Anything below y=6 collides with the footer. `bbox_inches="tight"` expands the saved area to include artists placed outside the axes, so out-of-bounds content appears in the output rather than being clipped — it fails silently and looks broken. This is documented in the script's `_finish()` docstring because it has already caused one rebuild.

```
content/training-resources/_assets/
    generate_diagrams.py
    diagrams/
        hierarchy-of-controls.png
        temperature-danger-zone.png
        ...
```

**Convention:** `kebab-case`, concept-named not unit-named — because diagrams are shared across units. `hierarchy-of-controls`, not `cpccwhs2001-fig-1`.

## Accessibility

**Every diagram needs alt text.** Not optional — RTOs delivering online have obligations under the *Disability Discrimination Act 1992* (Cth), and WCAG conformance is increasingly specified in government-funded delivery.

**Alt text must describe the information, not the picture.**

| Poor | Adequate |
|---|---|
| "Diagram of the hierarchy of controls" | "Inverted pyramid showing six levels of control from most to least effective: elimination, substitution, isolation, engineering, administrative, and PPE. Levels one to four protect regardless of worker behaviour; levels five and six depend on it." |

**[TO BUILD: an `alt-text.json` mapping every diagram slug to its alt text, consumed by the PDF and LMS build.]**

Also required:
- Minimum 8pt effective type at final print size
- Contrast ratios meeting WCAG AA
- No information conveyed by colour alone ✅ already satisfied

## Integration Into Documents

Markdown, for the PDF build to resolve:

```markdown
![Inverted pyramid showing six levels of control from most to least effective...](../_assets/diagrams/hierarchy-of-controls.png)

*Figure 1 — The hierarchy of controls. Apply in order; a lower level is only
acceptable where the higher levels are not reasonably practicable.*
```

**Rules:**
- Alt text is the description, not the filename
- Every figure gets a caption that adds information rather than repeating the title
- Number figures per document
- Reference the figure from the body text — an unreferenced image is decoration

## Open Questions for Liam

1. **Commissioned photography — worth the spend?** My view: not yet. Diagrams cover the teaching need for most units, and photography is the expensive part. Revisit after first revenue.

2. **SVG as well as PNG?** SVG scales cleanly and is smaller, but complicates the PDF pipeline and is trivially editable — which matters when a licensee should not be editing. **Recommend PNG only for distribution**, SVG retained internally if ever needed.

3. **Should diagrams carry the licensee's branding in co-branded builds?** **Recommend no.** Keep the AIIPD attribution burned in. It is your work, it travels with the image if extracted, and it reinforces provenance.

4. **Do you want a diagram in every unit, or only where it earns its place?** **Recommend the latter.** A forced diagram in a unit that does not need one is filler, and filler is visible.
