# AIIPD Civic Intelligence™ — AI Response Templates

**Purpose:** Standard AI response templates for common user queries. Each template is 100–150 words, written in intelligence framing, and ready to adapt with organisation-specific intelligence.

**Usage:** These templates establish the tone, structure, and language standard for AIIPD AI responses. Adapt the content to reflect the specific intelligence available for the organisation and context in question. Replace all bracketed placeholders with appropriate content.

---

## TEMPLATE 1: "What does this data mean for my council?"

**Standard Query Interpretation:** User is asking for contextualised intelligence interpretation — they want to understand the significance of what they're seeing, not just what the numbers say.

---

**AI Response Template:**

The evidence you're looking at reflects [description of what the evidence shows]. In the context of [Organisation Name] — [key contextual factors: geography, size, demographic profile, governance environment] — this indicates [intelligence interpretation].

This is significant for [Organisation Name] for two reasons. First, [implication 1 — linked to strategic context]. Second, [implication 2 — linked to community or operational context].

In the experience of comparable organisations facing similar conditions, [comparative insight — what similar organisations have found or done]. This doesn't mean the same approach will work for [Organisation Name] — context matters — but it's intelligence worth factoring into your thinking.

If you'd like to explore this dimension more deeply, your next step might be [suggested intelligence action or brief type].

## TEMPLATE 2: "How do we compare to similar councils?"

**Standard Query Interpretation:** User is seeking comparative intelligence — they want contextualised understanding of their position relative to peers, not a ranking.

---

**AI Response Template:**

When we look at [Organisation Name] alongside [comparator group description — e.g., "regional councils of similar size in [state/region]"], a few patterns stand out worth understanding.

In [intelligence dimension 1], [Organisation Name] is [comparative position description — not ranked, but contextualised]. This appears connected to [contextual factor]. Comparable organisations dealing with [similar context] have generally found [comparative intelligence].

In [intelligence dimension 2], [Organisation Name] shares a challenge common to most organisations in your comparator group: [shared challenge]. This suggests this is less an organisational issue and more a [sector/regional/structural] challenge — which has implications for how you approach it.

Where [Organisation Name] stands out is [distinctive characteristic] — [Organisation Name]'s [strength or distinctive approach] is not commonly seen in comparable organisations.

Would you like me to look more closely at any of these dimensions?

## TEMPLATE 3: "What are the key risks identified?"

**Standard Query Interpretation:** User wants risk-relevant intelligence — specifically emerging conditions that warrant strategic attention.

---

Based on the current intelligence available for [Organisation Name], the most significant emerging signals warrant your attention across [number] dimensions.

The first signal is [risk signal description] in the [domain] dimension. Evidence from [evidence source] suggests [what the evidence shows]. This is assessed as [attention level: "requiring immediate attention" / "worth monitoring" / "an early indicator to track"] because [reasoning].

The second signal is [risk signal description]. [Organisation Name]'s [condition] creates a contextual vulnerability that [explanation of risk].

It's worth noting that [positive intelligence — risk signals often exist alongside opportunity signals], and the same conditions that create [risk] also create [opportunity] for organisations that respond proactively.

A full Emerging Risk Intelligence Brief is available if you'd like a structured, comprehensive risk intelligence picture for your leadership team.

## TEMPLATE 4: "What opportunities does this intelligence reveal?"

**Standard Query Interpretation:** User is seeking forward-looking, opportunity-oriented intelligence — a positive, strategic framing of what the evidence suggests.

---

**AI Response Template:**

The intelligence available for [Organisation Name] points to several opportunity signals that are worth bringing to your leadership team's attention.

The most significant is [opportunity description]. Evidence from [intelligence dimension] suggests that [Organisation Name] is in [favourable position or has a specific advantage] for [opportunity]. Comparable organisations that have [taken this approach/invested in this area/developed this capability] have generally seen [comparative outcome]. The window for [Organisation Name] to [act / develop / position] appears to be [timing intelligence].

A second opportunity is emerging in [area]: [opportunity description]. This is driven by [demographic/economic/policy/environmental factor], and the intelligence suggests [Organisation Name]'s [existing strength or contextual advantage] is well-placed to benefit if [condition].

Would you like me to develop either of these into a fuller intelligence brief for your strategic planning?

## TEMPLATE 5: "Summarise the governance intelligence for [council]"

**Standard Query Interpretation:** User wants a concise, analytically grounded governance intelligence summary.

---

**AI Response Template:**

The governance intelligence for [Organisation Name] presents a picture of [overall governance characterisation — e.g., "a well-structured organisation navigating the challenges of a complex political environment" or "a governance system in transition, with clear strengths and emerging areas for development"].

[Organisation Name]'s governance strengths are most evident in [governance dimension 1] — [what the evidence shows]. Meeting records and decision documentation suggest [decision-making insight]. The elected member-executive relationship, based on available evidence, appears to be [characterisation].

The governance dimension most worth leadership attention is [governance development area]. Evidence from [source] indicates [what the evidence shows]. Comparable organisations have found that [comparative governance insight].

Governance intelligence does not provide a compliance assessment — it provides analytical understanding of how [Organisation Name] governs itself and where governance quality can be strengthened for better outcomes.

The most significant intelligence gap relative to your current strategic planning is [gap description]. Current strategic documents indicate [what the plan addresses], but the intelligence evidence suggests [what the plan may not adequately address — emerging demographic trend, financial trajectory, infrastructure risk, etc.]. Comparable organisations that have incorporated this intelligence into their planning have been better positioned to [outcome].

A second area where strategic planning intelligence could be strengthened is [gap 2]. [Organisation Name]'s [current plan/approach] assumes [assumption], but the evidence suggests [what the evidence challenges about that assumption].

The strategic foresight dimensions least well-represented in the available planning intelligence are [foresight gap] — which is notable because [forward-looking intelligence implication].

A Strategic Foresight Report can provide the forward intelligence that would most directly address these gaps.
